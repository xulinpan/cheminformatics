\subsection*{Abstract}
\small
Machine-learning methods for tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) increasingly replace binned intensity vectors with models reading individual peaks at full mass precision, and gains over binned baselines are often read as evidence for the richer representation. Such comparisons also change the encoder, the pooling rule, the acquisition information supplied and the effort spent on each arm. We separate these sources in MS/MS substructure prediction on an openly licensed corpus of $414{,}992$ spectra from GNPS, MassBank and MoNA spanning $66$ instrument types. Across $10{,}058$ held-out molecules and three seeds, an unconditioned binned baseline trails a full-precision peak-token model by $+0.0579$ macro AUPRC. Two interventions on the comparator alone remove that gap. Supplying the binned baseline with the acquisition covariates the peak-token arms already received raises it from $0.1130$ to $0.1418$. A prespecified search of its own, selected on the validation fold and never consulting fold 0, raises it to $0.2601 \pm 0.0008$ --- above every peak-token arm in every seed. Tuning is worth $+0.1183$, four times the metadata correction and more than every modelling effect here combined; its selected configuration sits on the grid boundary, so it is a lower bound. Contrasts not involving the baseline are unaffected: inside a fixed encoder, restoring full mass precision contributes $+0.0142$ ($95\%$ interval $+0.0097$ to $+0.0187$), the study's one single-factor intervention. We do not conclude that binned representations are superior --- the tuned baseline carries $6.2\times$ the parameters and the peak-token arms are untuned --- but that no ordering follows from a comparison of this kind, and that comparator construction must be controlled before a representation gain is attributed.
\normalsize

\hd{Scientific contribution}
\small
We provide three controls for attributing gains in MS/MS representation studies: metadata parity between comparators, a prespecified per-arm hyperparameter search, and a rounding control varying mass-axis precision inside a fixed peak-token encoder. On one five-arm ladder the first two together eliminate the apparent advantage of a full-precision peak-token model over a binned baseline, while the third isolates a mass-axis effect of $+0.0142$ that survives because no baseline enters it. The contribution is methodological: a reported gain of this kind can be an artefact of comparator construction rather than of representation.
\normalsize

\section*{Introduction}
Assigning a chemical structure to an MS/MS spectrum is a central challenge in untargeted metabolomics. Where the molecule appears in a reference library, the task can be approached by spectral matching, but many spectra acquired from biological extracts have no exact reference match \cite{hoffmann2022cosmic}. Computational annotation therefore relates a spectrum to candidate structures through in-silico fragmentation and scoring, as in MetFrag \cite{ruttkies2016metfrag}; prediction of structural fingerprints followed by database search, as in CSI:FingerID \cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid} and MIST \cite{goldman2023mist}; or learned spectral representations, as in MS2DeepScore \cite{huber2021ms2deepscore} and DreaMS \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}.

For much of the recent machine-learning literature, a spectrum has been discretised onto a fixed grid of bins and presented to a model as a dense vector. This convention originated in spectral library search, where bins wider than the instrument's mass error make matching robust to calibration drift. A $0.5$~Da bin spans roughly $1{,}700$~ppm at $\mz=300$, whereas a modern time-of-flight instrument can operate at approximately $5$~ppm. Such binning discards fine-scale information including mass defect, the difference between exact and nominal mass; for example, $\mathrm{C_5H_7^+}$ ($67.0542$) and $\mathrm{C_4H_3O^+}$ ($67.0178$) fall within the same $0.5$~Da bin despite distinct elemental compositions.

Modern systems increasingly avoid coarse binning. CSI:FingerID scores fragmentation trees over exact masses, MIST tokenises peaks by chemical formula, DreaMS operates on peak lists, and TransExION embeds nominal mass and mass defect in separate channels \cite{buithi2024transexion}. Recent work also benchmarks alternative MS/MS featurisations \cite{gine2026benchmarking} and examines how much mass resolution chemical-similarity prediction requires \cite{dejonge2026cross}.

These two studies are the most direct empirical statements available about what mass precision is worth, and they point in different directions. Gin\'e et al.\ \cite{gine2026benchmarking} compare adaptive binning and spectrum hashing against learned embeddings across a large grid of models for structure annotation, and report that retrieval degrades markedly as the mass tolerance is relaxed. Working on chemical-similarity prediction rather than structure annotation, de Jonge et al.\ \cite{dejonge2026cross} adopt $0.1$~Da bins and report that finer bins did not improve performance. The disagreement is itself a reason to ask the question in a form that admits a clean answer. Both establish that representation matters; neither isolates which part of a pipeline causes a particular gain, because a benchmark measures each featurisation bundled with the encoder that reads it and therefore estimates a joint effect at many points rather than separating it at one.

Two further strands bear directly on the present design. First, acquisition conditioning: collision energy, adduct, polarity and instrument determine which bonds break and which ions are observed, and models that receive this information as an explicit input are now common. Whether a comparator receives the same information is, as far as we are aware, not routinely reported. Second, multi-spectrum aggregation: a molecule is typically represented by several spectra acquired at different energies or in different adduct forms, and how those are combined is a modelling choice independent of how any single spectrum is encoded. Benchmarks such as MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym} have standardised evaluation and leakage control for this field, but the attribution question --- which component of a pipeline a reported gain belongs to --- is orthogonal to benchmarking and is not resolved by it.

The reason attribution is hard here is structural. A dense binned vector and a sparse, variable-length peak set require different input interfaces, so moving from one to the other commonly changes the representation and the encoder together. Additional differences enter through pooling, training protocol and acquisition metadata. A two-arm comparison therefore estimates the joint difference between complete pipelines, not the causal contribution of the spectral representation alone.

We address this attribution problem in a controlled MS/MS substructure-prediction setting. Our key control quantises the mass axis \emph{inside} the peak-token pipeline: peaks are snapped to the same $0.5$~Da grid used by the dense baseline, collisions are merged, and the resulting tokens pass through the same peak-set encoder used for full-precision peaks. This makes the M1R-to-M1 contrast a direct test of mass-axis precision while holding the peak-token architecture fixed. We also correct an acquisition-metadata imbalance in the original dense baseline. Finally, we include an attention-free token sensitivity arm and a multi-spectrum aggregation arm to place the mass-axis effect in context. Throughout, we distinguish contrasts that are single-factor interventions from those that remain composite.

This paper does not propose a state-of-the-art structure-prediction architecture, and it does not benchmark external systems. CSI:FingerID, MIST, DreaMS, MS2DeepScore and TransExION differ from this work and from each other in training data, prediction target, objective and architecture; a number produced here is not comparable to a number produced there. What the paper offers is a control strategy --- metadata parity and within-encoder mass quantisation --- and an empirical demonstration of how much a reported representation gain can move when those controls are applied.

\section*{Methods}
\hd{Corpora and splits}
Both corpora are drawn from the CASMI 2026 training corpus, an aggregation of eleven public and in-house MS/MS libraries whose \texttt{ingest\_lib} field records the source library.

The primary corpus is the openly licensed subset contributed by GNPS, MassBank and MoNA: $414{,}992$ spectra over $52{,}428$ structures, spanning $66$ instrument types and $112$ adducts. After filtering, $372{,}200$ spectra over $50{,}287$ structures and $24{,}627{,}256$ peaks remain for modelling. We distinguish these source and retained counts throughout. All primary results are reported on this corpus.

The secondary corpus is a contiguous slice of two of the file's twenty-one row groups, containing $262{,}144$ spectra. Because the file is ordered by source library, this slice is not a random sample: it is $99.0\%$ one in-house library and $1.0\%$ another, and $99\%$ of spectra come from a single instrument (timsTOF), across two instrument types and sixteen adducts. We use it as a deliberately contrasting sensitivity corpus. It is not an external validation set and not an independent replication: it is drawn from the same aggregate resource, processed by the same code, and is not redistributable.

Spectra were retained if precursor $\mz<2{,}000$ and the reported precursor error was within $20$~ppm. Peaks were retained above $0.1\%$ of the base peak and below the precursor plus $1.5$~Da, capped at the $256$ most intense, with $\mz$ held in double precision throughout preprocessing.

\hd{Molecular identity and the fold assignment}
Molecular identity is the first block of the InChIKey, the $14$-character skeleton hash. This identifier ignores stereochemistry, isotopic labelling and protonation state, so stereoisomers, tautomers recorded as distinct records, salt forms and charge states of one compound collapse to a single identifier and are therefore assigned to a single fold. The same identifier also merges records duplicated across source libraries, which is the case that matters most here: GNPS, MassBank and MoNA overlap, and a compound present in two of them must not be split between fitting and evaluation. Structures are additionally represented by a canonical SMILES string, used for scaffold computation and for reporting, but identity is defined by the InChIKey block alone.

Structures were then assigned to five folds by grouping generic Murcko scaffolds, with acyclic structures falling back to grouping by their own identifier, and fold sizes balanced greedily. Folds 2--4 were used for fitting, fold 1 for model selection and fold 0 for final evaluation. This design follows the generalisation-demanding practice used in MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}, while retaining source-library selection specific to the present study.

Scaffold grouping is stricter than a random split over spectra, and stricter than a split over structures, because it prevents a model from being evaluated on a molecule whose scaffold it has already seen. It is not equivalent to chemical-space independence. Two molecules with different generic Murcko scaffolds may still be close neighbours by any pharmacophoric or fragment-level measure, and BRICS fragments in particular recur across scaffolds by construction. A split defined on a graph edit distance between structures, as used by MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}, is stricter again; we did not adopt it here because our corpora are selected by source library rather than drawn from that benchmark.

\hd{Split-integrity audit}
We verified the split rather than asserting it. On the primary corpus, across $50{,}287$ structures and $372{,}200$ retained spectra: no molecular identifier occurred in more than one fold ($0$ of $50{,}287$); no scaffold group occurred in more than one fold ($0$ of $19{,}648$ groups, of which $7{,}456$ are acyclic structures keyed by their own identifier); every spectrum inherited the fold of its structure, with none unassigned. Fold sizes are $10{,}058$, $10{,}058$, $10{,}057$, $10{,}057$ and $10{,}057$ structures. The same audit on the sensitivity corpus is likewise clean: $0$ of $43{,}536$ identifiers and $0$ of $13{,}620$ scaffold groups span folds.

Fold 0 of the primary corpus contains $10{,}058$ structures and all $10{,}058$ are evaluated. Fold 0 of the sensitivity corpus contains $8{,}708$ structures, of which $16$ are structures recoverable from the CASMI competition test set by exact spectral duplicate matching; those are excluded from both fitting and evaluation, leaving the $8{,}692$ reported. No such structures are present in the primary corpus, which is one further reason to treat it as primary. The audit is reproduced by \texttt{scripts/split\_integrity\_audit.py}.

\hd{Structural targets}
Each structure was decomposed with BRICS and represented by the presence or absence of each of the $512$ most frequent attachment-labelled fragments, with the vocabulary fitted on the training folds only. On the primary corpus the target matrix is $99.3\%$ zero with a median of four positive targets per molecule. A total of $433$ targets have at least five held-out positives, at a mean prevalence of $0.0085$, which is the chance level for macro AUPRC. The sensitivity corpus is less sparse ($97.1\%$ zero, median $17$ positive targets, $477$ scored targets, mean prevalence $0.0093$). Because the targets are highly imbalanced, macro AUPRC is the primary metric; macro AUROC, micro AUPRC and Brier score are secondary summaries.

\hd{Five-arm ablation ladder}
The five arms are ordered as M0, M1D, M1R, M1 and M2. The ladder decomposes the observed pipeline difference, but not every adjacent contrast is a single-factor intervention. M1R-to-M1 and M2-to-M1 change one component; M0-to-M1D and M1D-to-M1R change several simultaneously and are labelled composite wherever they appear.

\emph{M0, metadata-matched dense baseline.} Each spectrum is projected onto $2{,}048$ fragment bins and $2{,}048$ precursor-minus-fragment bins of width $0.5$~Da over $[0,1024)$, with square-root intensity accumulated within bins and the concatenated $4{,}096$-dimensional vector $L_2$-normalised. A two-layer perceptron encodes the vector. Collision energy, adduct, polarity and instrument embeddings are concatenated to the dense representation. This gives M0 access to the same acquisition variables as the peak-token arms, although the conditioning mechanism is not identical: the token encoder injects them through adaptive layer normalisation. We therefore describe the corrected comparator as metadata \emph{matched}, not as mechanically identical in conditioning, and we treat the difference in mechanism as a limitation of the M1R-to-M0 contrast rather than a property of it.

\emph{M1R, rounded peak-token control.} Each fragment mass $m$ is assigned to a cell by $\lfloor m/w \rfloor$, with $w=0.5$~Da, and moved to the cell centre. Peaks sharing a cell are merged by accumulating square-root intensity, matching the accumulator used by M0. The precursor is snapped to the same grid before the neutral loss is computed, preventing fine precursor mass from leaking back into the token representation through the subtraction $m^{\mathrm{prec}}-m$. The resulting tokens pass through the same peak-token encoder used by M1. M1R and M1 have identical parameter counts ($712{,}336$) and differ only by whether mass quantisation is active. This is the principal representation control in the study.

\emph{M1D, attention-free token sensitivity arm.} M1D uses the same rounded peak masses and acquisition variables as M1R, but removes self-attention from the token blocks. Because a learned pooling token cannot aggregate independent position-wise tokens in the absence of attention, M1D pools peaks by a masked mean. Removing attention also frees parameters, so its feed-forward layers were widened, yielding $714{,}528$ parameters versus $712{,}336$ for M1R. M1D therefore tests whether the attention-bearing configuration improves on an approximately capacity-matched attention-free token model. It does \emph{not} isolate a self-attention effect, because pooling and feed-forward width change with it.

\emph{M1, full-precision peak-token model.} M1 is identical to M1R except that mass quantisation is disabled, so the model receives the instrument-reported mass precision. The M1-to-M1R contrast isolates the mass-axis precision intervention within a fixed encoder, pooling rule, training protocol and parameter count.

\emph{M2, multi-spectrum aggregation.} M2 is identical to M1 except that the mean over a molecule's spectra is replaced by the measurement model specified below. The encoder, the mass axis, the peak pooling, the training protocol and the target heads are unchanged. The contrast is nonetheless composite rather than single-factor, and we label it so throughout: the measurement model replaces M1's linear output head with a learned per-target prior, covariate-dependent scaling and a logistic--Gaussian output map, all of which act even on a molecule represented by a single spectrum, where there is nothing to aggregate. Sect.~\ref{sec:agg} reports the stratification that separates the two components empirically.

\hd{Peak encoding}
Each peak is encoded as a token. For a peak at $\mz=m$ in a spectrum with precursor mass $m^{\mathrm{prec}}$, we form a multi-scale sinusoidal embedding
\begin{equation}
\varphi_L(m)=\bigl[\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_1),\cos(2\pi m/\lambda_1),\ldots,\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_L),\cos(2\pi m/\lambda_L)\bigr]^\top,
\end{equation}
with $L=32$ wavelengths spaced geometrically from $2$~mDa to $10^3$~Da, applied to both fragment mass and neutral loss. The token also receives the explicit defect $\delta=m-\mathrm{round}(m)$, square-root and log-transformed intensity, and intensity rank. At a wavelength of $2$~mDa and $\mz=342$ the phase exceeds $10^6$, so the phase is evaluated in double precision and reduced modulo $2\pi$ before casting to single precision. Tokens are encoded by a four-block Transformer of width $128$ with eight heads, with acquisition covariates entering each block through adaptive layer normalisation.

\hd{Acquisition covariates}
For spectrum $k$ of molecule $i$, the covariate vector $c_{ik}\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$ is produced by a small encoder from learned embeddings of adduct, polarity and instrument family, a six-knot spline basis in harmonised collision energy, the log precursor mass, and an explicit indicator for missing collision energy. Energies reported as normalised collision energy are converted to eV by $\mathrm{eV}=\mathrm{NCE}\cdot \mz/500$; energies in unrecognised units are treated as missing rather than placed on the eV scale. Missing metadata are therefore represented explicitly rather than imputed, and the same $c_{ik}$ is supplied to every arm.

\hd{Multi-spectrum aggregation in M2}
Let molecule $i$ have spectra $x_{i1},\dots,x_{iK_i}$ with encoder outputs $h_{ik}\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ and covariate vectors $c_{ik}$. M2 aggregates at two levels, and we state both because the second is where the arm's parameters lie.

\emph{Acquisition-dependent reliability.} For each target $j\in\{1,\dots,512\}$ the covariates produce three quantities by rank-one affine maps of $c_{ik}$,
\begin{align}
\alpha_j(c_{ik}) &= \alpha^0_j + a_j^\top c_{ik}, \\
\beta_j(c_{ik}) &= \mathrm{softplus}\bigl(\beta^0_j + b_j^\top c_{ik}\bigr) + \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-2},\\
\log \sigma_j(c_{ik}) &= s^0_j + s_j^\top c_{ik}, \qquad \text{clamped to } [-4, 4],
\end{align}
an offset, a sensitivity and a noise scale. Positivity of $\beta_j$ is enforced by the softplus and the floor $\varepsilon$; positivity of $\sigma_j$ by the exponential, and the clamp bounds it away from zero and infinity. The per-spectrum precision for target $j$ is
\begin{equation}
w_{ikj} = \beta_j(c_{ik})^2/\sigma_j(c_{ik})^2,
\label{eq:prec}
\end{equation}
clamped above at $10^3$. Note that $w_{ikj}$ depends on the acquisition covariates only, not on $h_{ik}$, so it can be evaluated before the spectra are pooled.

\emph{Representation-level pooling.} The molecule representation is a normalised weighted mean over the observed spectra, using the precision of Eq.~\eqref{eq:prec} averaged over targets,
\begin{equation}
\bar w_{ik} = \tfrac{1}{512}\textstyle\sum_j w_{ikj}, \qquad
h_i = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K_i} \bar w_{ik}\, h_{ik}}{\sum_{l=1}^{K_i} \bar w_{il}} .
\label{eq:hpool}
\end{equation}
The weights are non-negative and, after the division, sum to one; masked positions contribute zero. M1 and the other arms use the same expression with $\bar w_{ik}\equiv 1$, that is, a masked mean.

\emph{Evidence-level pooling.} A per-spectrum evidence logit $\ell_{ikj}=(\,W h_{ik} + b\,)_j$ is combined across spectra by Gaussian-conjugate pooling. With the prior mean $\mu_{ij} = (\,W' h_i + b'\,)_j$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:hpool} and a learned per-target prior precision $\tau_j^{-2}$,
\begin{align}
S^{(1)}_{ij} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K_i} \frac{\beta_j(c_{ik})\bigl(\ell_{ikj}-\alpha_j(c_{ik})\bigr)}{\sigma_j(c_{ik})^2}, &
S^{(2)}_{ij} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K_i} w_{ikj}, \\
v_{ij} &= \bigl(\tau_j^{-2} + S^{(2)}_{ij}\bigr)^{-1}, &
m_{ij} &= v_{ij}\bigl(\mu_{ij}\tau_j^{-2} + S^{(1)}_{ij}\bigr),
\end{align}
and the reported probability uses the logistic--Gaussian approximation
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{logit}\,\hat p_{ij} = m_{ij}\big/\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{\pi}{8} v_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
Because $S^{(1)}$ and $S^{(2)}$ are sums over spectra, a molecule's spectra need not be processed in one pass: statistics accumulated over chunks give the same posterior, so no cap on $K_i$ is required. All aggregation parameters ($\alpha^0,a,\beta^0,b,s^0,s,\tau,W,b,W',b'$) are trained jointly with the encoder by the same objective; none is fitted post hoc.

\hd{Bin-width sweep}
To examine the precision scale continuously, we repeated the M1R construction at $w\in\{0.25,0.1,0.05,0.01\}$~Da, holding the encoder, parameter count, training schedule and seed fixed. Together with $w=0.5$ and full precision, this produces six mass-resolution settings. The sweep was run on the sensitivity corpus only and each intermediate width was trained once; it is exploratory except at its endpoints.

\hd{Training protocol}
Every arm was trained for 15 epochs with AdamW, a one-cycle learning-rate schedule peaking at $10^{-3}$, batches of 16 molecules, mixed precision and gradient clipping at 1.0. The objective was binary cross-entropy over the 512 BRICS-presence targets plus an auxiliary negative-binomial term on 88 functional-group counts. Checkpoints were selected by validation macro AUPRC on fold 1. The five ladder arms on the primary corpus and four arms on the sensitivity corpus were each trained under seeds $\{2026,7,13\}$, for 27 ladder runs in total, in addition to the single-seed sweep runs.

All arms were implemented for this study in PyTorch 2.11.0 (CUDA 12.8) under Python 3.14, with NumPy 2.4.4, pandas 2.3.3 and RDKit 2026.3.6, and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 3070. M0 reproduces the binned convention described in the Introduction rather than any particular published system, and no external implementation was used as a comparator; every difference reported here is between arms of one codebase, which is what makes the within-architecture controls necessary rather than optional.

\hd{Hyperparameter selection, and a known asymmetry}
No hyperparameter search was performed for any arm. This is not a symmetric choice and we state it plainly: the schedule above was selected for the peak-token Transformer and inherited unchanged by the dense baseline, so M0 and the token arms are not equidistant from their respective optima. Because the central claim of this paper concerns comparator fairness, a comparator that is merely untuned is a weaker instrument than the argument requires. The metadata correction reported below, worth more than any contrast in the study, is direct evidence that M0 was not near its ceiling.

We therefore prespecify a modest M0-only tuning experiment. The scaffold split is fixed and unchanged; folds 2--4 are used for fitting and fold 1 for model selection; fold 0 is not consulted at any point during selection. The grid varies learning rate $\in\{3\times10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 3\times10^{-3}\}$, hidden width $\in\{256, 512, 1024\}$ and dropout $\in\{0.1, 0.3\}$, all other settings held at the values above. The configuration with the best fold-1 macro AUPRC is then trained under the three seeds and evaluated once on fold 0. The purpose is to test whether the residual M1R-to-M0 difference survives a stronger dense comparator; if the tuned baseline changes the decomposition, every contrast involving M0 must be recomputed and reported against the tuned baseline, and the contrasts not involving M0 --- M1-to-M1R and M2-to-M1 --- are unaffected by construction. The experiment was run and its outcome is reported in Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned}; we retain the design here in the prospective form in which it was fixed.

Selection runs evaluate the validation fold only. The driver refuses to proceed if a
selection run has produced a held-out score, so the search cannot be contaminated by
fold 0 even accidentally. As a check that the grid does what it claims, it
reproduces the incumbent configuration's validation score exactly at the
incumbent's own settings.

\hd{A matched search for the peak-token arms}
Tuning one side of a contrast and not the other reverses an unfairness rather than
removing it. The same grid is therefore specified for M1R, with the width axis
mapped to the knob that carries capacity in that arm --- the feed-forward width of
the peak-token blocks --- and $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ held at $128$ so that the
representation width is unchanged and the search varies capacity rather than the
interface. Learning rate and dropout ranges are identical, selection is on fold 1,
and fold 0 is not consulted. The search is specified here because it is the
experiment that would settle the comparison of Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned}; at the time
of writing it has been launched but not completed, and no result from it is reported
anywhere in this paper.

\hd{Evaluation and three sources of uncertainty}
Macro AUPRC is reported over targets with at least five held-out positives. Three distinct sources of variability bear on the contrasts, and we report them separately rather than combining them into one interval.

\emph{(i) Seed variability.} Each arm is trained three times under different initialisations. We report the standard deviation of each contrast across seeds, and we check every claimed ordering within each seed rather than reading it off the means.

\emph{(ii) Target-resampling uncertainty.} For paired contrasts, each target's average precision is first averaged over the three seeds; per-target differences are then computed and their mean is resampled over targets with $5{,}000$ bootstrap draws. We call the resulting intervals \emph{target-resampling intervals}. They describe heterogeneity across the scored target set at a fixed held-out set of molecules. They do \emph{not} describe uncertainty arising from which molecules were held out, and BRICS targets are not statistically independent because fragments co-occur, so these intervals are narrower than a fully independent resampling would give.

\emph{(iii) Test-set uncertainty over structures.} To quantify the sampling of held-out molecules we add a \emph{scaffold-cluster bootstrap}. Scaffold groups, not molecules or spectra, are the resampling unit, because molecules sharing a generic Murcko scaffold were assigned to a fold together and are not exchangeable with one another. Whole scaffold groups are drawn with replacement, the full macro AUPRC is recomputed per arm within each resample over the scored targets, and the contrasts are formed inside the resample so that pairing is preserved. We use $2{,}000$ draws over the $3{,}784$ scaffold clusters that partition the $10{,}058$ held-out structures.

Neither bootstrap is a confidence interval for the population of all possible MS/MS tasks. The target-resampling interval covers the sampling of targets at a fixed test set; the scaffold-cluster interval covers the sampling of structures at a fixed target set; seed spread covers initialisation. We interpret all three descriptively.

\section*{Results}
Each contrast below is labelled \onefactor{} where the arms differ in exactly one component, and \composite{} where they differ in more than one. Causal language is used only for the former.

\hd{1. Metadata matching substantially reduces the apparent two-arm gap}
The initial dense baseline did not use collision energy, adduct, polarity or instrument variables, whereas the peak-token models did. Adding the same acquisition variables to M0 by concatenating their embedding to the dense spectral vector raises M0 from $0.1130$ to $0.1418$ macro AUPRC, an improvement of $+0.0288$. Consequently the apparent M1-to-M0 advantage falls from $+0.0579$ to $+0.0291$. About half of the original two-arm difference was attributable to unequal metadata availability rather than to mass precision or peak-token modelling.

At this point in the study the metadata correction was the largest single quantity measured, and it already belonged to the construction of the comparator rather than to any modelling choice under test. Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned} reports a second intervention on the comparator that is four times larger again. Because M0 and the peak-token arms inject the metadata through different mechanisms, we describe the corrected comparator as metadata matched, not as mechanically identical in conditioning. Table~\ref{tab:arms} summarises the five arms under the corrected baseline.

Every contrast in Sects.~2--4 below is measured against this metadata-matched but
\emph{untuned} baseline, which is the comparator a conventional study would use. None
of them survives Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned} in the form given here, and we report them in
this order because the sequence is the finding: each successive correction to the
comparator moved the answer more than the modelling choices being compared.

\hd{2. Restoring mass precision within a fixed encoder gives a reproducible gain (\onefactor)}
Against the metadata-matched baseline, M1 reaches $0.1709$, a paired target-level gain of $+0.0291$ ($95\%$ target-resampling interval $+0.0208$ to $+0.0371$), with $72.5\%$ of targets improved. The rounding control divides that numerical difference into $+0.0149$ for M1R minus M0 and $+0.0142$ for M1 minus M1R.

The latter is the study's one clean single-factor intervention. M1R and M1 are identical in encoder, pooling, parameter count, schedule and seed set, and differ only in whether the mass axis is quantised to the baseline's grid. The gain is $+0.0142$ ($+0.0097$ to $+0.0187$), improving $64.9\%$ of targets, and it is the most reproducible contrast across seeds in the study, with a seed standard deviation of $0.0004$ against $0.0013$ for M1R minus M0 and $0.0009$ for aggregation. Mass precision therefore carries predictive information that the binned representation removes, and the size of that information is measured here without confounding by architecture.

\hd{3. The rounded token-encoder package contributes a numerically similar but composite increment (\composite)}
The M1R-to-M0 difference of $+0.0149$ ($+0.0080$ to $+0.0221$; $66.7\%$ of targets) is broader than an encoder change. The two arms differ in input interface (dense vector against per-peak tokens), in network family (perceptron against Transformer), in peak pooling, and in the mechanism by which acquisition covariates are injected (concatenation against adaptive layer normalisation). We therefore call it the \emph{rounded token-encoder package} difference and do not attribute it to any one of those components.

Its numerical similarity to the mass-axis increment supports a negative statement rather than a positive one: after metadata matching, the residual advantage of the full-precision peak-token model is not dominated by mass resolution. It does not establish that architecture and mass precision have equal causal effects, and the two cannot be ordered reliably --- their difference is $+0.0019$, $+0.0015$ and $-0.0012$ across the three seeds, changing sign.

\hd{4. The present attention comparison does not isolate a self-attention effect (\composite)}
M1D minus M0 is $+0.0172$ ($+0.0102$ to $+0.0242$), and M1R minus M1D is $-0.0023$ ($-0.0066$ to $+0.0020$), an interval containing zero, with $54.3\%$ of targets improved and a positive mean in only one seed of three. The attention-bearing rounded-token model is therefore not shown to outperform the approximately capacity-matched attention-free one in this comparison.

We do not read the negative mean as evidence that attention is harmful, and we do not read the result as an attention effect at all: M1D and M1R differ in the attention operator, in peak pooling and in feed-forward width together. The defensible statement is narrow --- the tested attention-bearing configuration was not necessary to reach the rounded-token performance observed here --- and the experiment that would price the operator is specified in Methods and has not been run.

\hd{5. The measurement model contributes an additional gain, only part of which is aggregation (\composite)}
\hdlab{5}{sec:agg}
Replacing the masked mean over a molecule's spectra with the measurement model of Eqs.~(2)--(8) is worth $+0.0179$ ($+0.0136$ to $+0.0223$; $63.7\%$ of targets).

This is not a pure aggregation effect, and stratifying the held-out set by the
number of spectra per molecule shows why. On the $3{,}443$ molecules represented by
a \emph{single} spectrum --- where there is nothing to pool --- M2 still exceeds M1
by $+0.0085$. The gain rises to $+0.0101$ on the $2{,}137$ molecules with two
spectra and $+0.0143$ on the $4{,}478$ with three or four. The single-spectrum
increment is attributable to the measurement model's other components: at $K_i=1$
Eqs.~(7)--(9) still apply a learned per-target prior, covariate-dependent scaling
and the logistic--Gaussian output map, none of which M1's linear head has. Roughly
half of the reported $+0.0179$ is therefore calibration rather than multi-spectrum
pooling, and the contrast is composite.

The monotone trend across strata is consistent with a genuine aggregation component
on top of that, and it carries a second implication: the evaluation uses at most
four spectra per molecule, while $2{,}755$ of the $10{,}058$ held-out molecules have
more available. The reported figure is therefore measured under a cap that
Eqs.~(7)--(8) do not require, and probably understates what the pooling contributes.

It exceeds the M1R-to-M0 package in all three seeds by $+0.0009$, $+0.0030$ and $+0.0049$, and the mass-axis intervention in all three by $+0.0028$, $+0.0045$ and $+0.0037$; the same direction against the package holds in all three seeds on the sensitivity corpus. With three seeds per corpus we treat this as a consistent empirical ordering in these runs rather than an established general ranking. Its relevance to the paper's argument is that a component with nothing to do with spectral representation produces an effect of the same magnitude as the representation change being studied.

The mechanism is underfitting, not regularisation. The untuned baseline ends training
at a presence loss of $0.01885$ against the tuned arm's $0.01084$: the tuned model
fits the training data better \emph{and} generalises better, with a smaller relative

rising at the final epoch under the shared schedule.

The cause is traceable to the design, which is the part that generalises beyond this
codebase. The binned encoder's hidden width was not chosen for a $4{,}096$-dimensional
input; it was set to twice $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, the peak-token encoder's width. The
comparator's only capacity parameter was a function of the treatment's
hyperparameter, and the learning-rate schedule was likewise selected for the
treatment's optimisation behaviour. A binned-versus-peak comparison can therefore be
biased before any data is seen, by inheritance rather than by intent, and this is
checkable in any codebase in minutes.

\hd{7. Peak absorption quantifies what coarse binning removes}
On the primary corpus's held-out spectra, $23.6\%$ of the $3{,}797{,}166$ peaks fall into a $0.5$~Da bin already occupied by another peak from the same spectrum and are therefore absorbed. The corresponding fraction on the sensitivity corpus's held-out spectra is $34.4\%$. At $0.01$~Da the reported held-out absorption fractions fall to $2.9\%$ and $6.5\%$ respectively. Absorption is reported throughout on each corpus's own held-out spectra; the same quantity computed over all retained spectra is $37.5\%$ rather than $34.4\%$ on the sensitivity corpus, and we quote the held-out figure because it matches the set on which the effects are measured.

A merged peak also loses compositional discrimination: enumerating plausible CHNOS compositions within $10$~ppm places $328$ candidate fragment formulae inside a single $0.5$~Da bin at $\mz=300$, consistent with the dependence of formula ambiguity on mass tolerance described by Kind and Fiehn \cite{kind2007seven}.

The corpus that loses more peaks at $0.5$~Da is also the corpus in which the mass-axis increment is larger ($34.4\%$ and $+0.0268$ versus $23.6\%$ and $+0.0142$). This association is descriptive and does not identify a mechanism; with two corpora it cannot. One plausible explanation is that the sensitivity corpus is almost entirely high-resolution timsTOF data whereas the primary corpus spans instruments of varying resolution, but that remains a hypothesis until the mass-axis increment is stratified directly by instrument resolution.

Two features of this table bear on how the paper's claims should be read. First,
the mass-axis intervention and the aggregation intervention are well behaved under

\section*{Discussion}
The main result is that a reported advantage of this kind can be an artefact of the comparator. A two-arm comparison between the original unconditioned binned baseline and the full-precision peak-token model yields $+0.0579$ macro AUPRC. Two interventions on the comparator alone, neither touching the treatment, remove it. Supplying the dense baseline with the acquisition covariates the peak-token models already had accounts for $+0.0288$. Giving it its own hyperparameter search accounts for $+0.1183$ more, and reverses the ordering: every peak-token arm then sits below the baseline in every seed.

Neither intervention is exotic. Metadata parity is a matter of passing the same
inputs to both sides. A per-arm hyperparameter search is standard practice that was
simply not applied here, and its omission was not neutral, because the shared
schedule and the comparator's width both came from the treatment arm. The
uncomfortable implication is that a study can satisfy the usual expectations ---
identical data, identical splits, identical objective, identical training budget,
three seeds --- and still produce an ordering that inverts under a control it did
not run.

Within the peak-token architecture, the rounding control gives the study's cleanest contrast: restoring full mass precision contributes $+0.0142$, measured with the encoder, pooling, parameter count and schedule held fixed. Mass precision is therefore not unimportant --- it carries real predictive information, measured here without architectural confounding, and it is the most reproducible effect in the study across seeds, targets and structures alike.

The remaining $+0.0149$ should not be described as an encoder effect. M1R and M0 differ in input interface, network family, peak pooling and conditioning mechanism simultaneously. The robust conclusion is therefore not that encoder and mass axis are equal, but that the apparent full-precision advantage is not dominated by mass resolution once the comparator receives the same acquisition variables.

The attention-free sensitivity arm supports a similarly narrow reading. M1R does not outperform M1D, but the arms differ in pooling and feed-forward width in addition to the attention operator. The experiment shows that the tested Transformer-style attention-bearing configuration is not necessary to obtain the rounded-token performance observed here; it does not identify the isolated effect of self-attention, and we have specified the ablation that would.

These distinctions matter because the purpose of the study is attribution. When representation, interface, architecture, pooling and metadata availability move together, a performance increment belongs to the whole package unless the design contains an intervention that changes only one component. The rounding control satisfies that requirement for mass-axis precision; the aggregation arm satisfies it for the tested aggregation module. The metadata correction establishes a separate and practically important point: comparator \emph{inputs} can account for a performance difference as large as the modelling changes being studied.

\hd{Relation to other work}
The systems this work sits alongside are not benchmarked here, and could not be without a different study design. CSI:FingerID \cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid}, MIST \cite{goldman2023mist}, DreaMS \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}, MS2DeepScore \cite{huber2021ms2deepscore} and TransExION \cite{buithi2024transexion} differ from this work and from one another in training corpus, prediction target, objective and architecture; a macro AUPRC over BRICS fragments is not comparable to a retrieval rank or a similarity score. This study therefore does not establish why any of them outperforms its own baseline.

What it offers those systems is a control strategy and an empirical warning. Featurisation benchmarks such as Gin\'e et al.\ \cite{gine2026benchmarking} measure each representation bundled with the encoder that reads it; that is the right design for choosing a pipeline and the wrong one for attributing a gain, and the rounding control is a cheap way to add the missing arm. Benchmarks such as MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym} have standardised splits and leakage control, which is a different axis from attribution and complementary to it: a leakage-controlled split makes a number trustworthy, while metadata parity and a within-encoder control make it interpretable. For metadata-conditioned models specifically, our result implies that reporting what the comparator was told is as important as reporting what the model was told. For multi-spectrum aggregation, the $+0.0179$ contributed by the measurement model here is a reminder that a component unrelated to spectral representation can produce an effect of the same size as the representation change under study, and should not be left uncontrolled in a comparison whose conclusion concerns representation.

\hd{Interpretation of the sensitivity corpus}
The contrasting corpus changes the acquisition regime substantially, which is why it is informative, but it is not an independent replication. The larger mass-axis increment on the nearly all-timsTOF corpus is compatible with the hypothesis that preserving reported mass precision is more valuable when the instrument genuinely supplies high-resolution measurements. That explanation is plausible and untested here; the appropriate next analysis stratifies the M1-minus-M1R effect by instrument-resolution class using the saved held-out predictions, which requires no retraining.

The bin-width sweep is likewise suggestive rather than definitive. It indicates that a $0.01$~Da grid recovers much of, but not all of, the full-precision advantage on the sensitivity corpus. This differs below $0.1$~Da from the result reported by de Jonge et al.\ \cite{dejonge2026cross}. The tasks, architectures and training stochasticity differ, and the present sweep has one seed at each intermediate width, so neither study alone settles a universal sufficient mass resolution.

\hd{Limitations}
\emph{Experimental design.} Three seeds per ladder arm are sufficient to show the observed seed-to-seed pattern but not to establish rankings among small effects. The dense baseline has now been searched; the peak-token arms have not, so the comparison in Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned} is unfair in the opposite direction to the original and supports no ordering claim. The tuned baseline is also not capacity matched, at $6.2\times$ the parameters of the peak-token arms, and its selected configuration sits on the grid boundary in both axes, so it is a lower bound rather than an optimum. The matched search is specified and incomplete.

\emph{Inference.} The target-resampling bootstrap does not capture uncertainty in which structures were held out; the scaffold-cluster bootstrap addresses that but holds the target set fixed. BRICS targets are correlated because fragments co-occur, so target-resampling intervals are narrower than fully independent resampling would give. Neither interval covers scaffold-split choice or the population of MS/MS tasks.

\emph{Ablation.} M1D-to-M1R is not a pure attention contrast, because pooling and feed-forward width change with the operator. M1R-to-M0 is composite in four respects at once, including the mechanism by which metadata are injected; a dense baseline conditioned through adaptive normalisation rather than concatenation would test whether the $+0.0149$ depends on that mechanism. M2-to-M1 is composite too: the measurement model changes the output head as well as the pooling, and about half its increment is present on molecules with a single spectrum. Only M1-to-M1R changes one component.

\emph{Aggregation under a cap.} Evaluation uses at most four spectra per molecule,
although $2{,}755$ of the $10{,}058$ held-out molecules have more, and the
measurement-model gain rises monotonically with the number of spectra used. The
reported figure is therefore a lower bound on what pooling contributes, and the
claim in Methods that the additive sufficient statistics impose no cap on $K_i$
describes the estimator rather than the experiment run here.

\emph{Data.} The secondary corpus is a deliberately contrasting in-house slice of the same aggregate resource, not an external or independent dataset. The instrument-resolution explanation for the difference between corpora is not tested directly.

\emph{Scope.} The endpoint is BRICS substructure prediction over 512 frequent fragments, not candidate ranking, full molecular fingerprints or de-novo structure elucidation. The conclusions do not establish performance in candidate retrieval or structure elucidation, and transfer to those endpoints remains to be demonstrated.

\emph{Reproducibility.} The primary corpus is assembled from openly licensed sources and its selection is scripted, but the in-house sensitivity corpus is not redistributable, so that arm of the study cannot be reproduced by a third party from public data.

\section*{Conclusion}

This study reports three findings at different levels.

First, the apparent advantage of a full-precision peak-token model over a binned
baseline did not survive two corrections to the comparator, neither of which touched
the treatment. On the primary corpus an unconditioned $0.5$~Da binned baseline
trails the peak-token model by $+0.0579$ macro AUPRC. Supplying the baseline with
collision energy, adduct, polarity and instrument raises it from $0.1130$ to
$0.1418$. Giving it a prespecified hyperparameter search of its own raises it to
$0.2601 \pm 0.0008$, above every peak-token arm in every seed. We draw no conclusion
about which representation is better: the tuned baseline is not capacity matched and
the peak-token arms have not been searched. We draw the conclusion that this
comparison does not determine an ordering.

Second, the contrasts that never involve the comparator are unaffected, and they are
the ones a within-architecture design was built to protect. Restoring full mass
precision inside a fixed peak-token encoder contributes $+0.0142$, with the encoder,
pooling rule, parameter count and schedule held fixed; it is the study's only
single-factor intervention and the most reproducible effect in it, across seeds,
targets and held-out structures alike. Mass precision carries real predictive
information, and that statement stands independently of how the binned baseline is
built, because no binned baseline enters it. The measurement model contributes
$+0.0179$ on the same comparator-free footing, but that figure bundles
multi-spectrum pooling with a change of output head and is reported as composite.

Third, comparator construction dominated every modelling effect this study set out to
measure. The metadata correction was worth $+0.0288$ and the tuning correction
$+0.1183$, against $+0.0142$ for the mass axis and $+0.0179$ for aggregation. The
comparator's capacity was moreover inherited from the treatment --- the binned
encoder's hidden width was twice the peak-token encoder's model dimension, and the
learning-rate schedule was chosen for the treatment's optimisation behaviour --- so
the bias was present in the design before any data was seen.

The practical consequence concerns experimental design rather than model ranking. A
gain measured against a binned baseline is evidence about the spectral representation
only to the extent that the baseline received the same inputs, the same search effort
and capacity chosen for its own architecture. Where the claim is about representation,
it should rest on a contrast in which the representation alone moves, inside a fixed
encoder. These conclusions are specific to this endpoint, these corpora and these
architectures.

The two controls are additionally released as \texttt{msms-controls}, a NumPy-only library under the MIT licence, so that the rounding control and the conditioning-parity check can be applied to other models without installing this project's training stack. Results in this paper correspond to a tagged release of that repository. A DOI-bearing archive of that release has not yet been deposited, so no persistent identifier is quoted here.\todoarchive

\emph{Environment.} \texttt{requirements-lock.txt} pins every dependency at the versions used for the reported runs (PyTorch 2.11.0+cu128, Python 3.14, NumPy 2.4.4, pandas 2.3.3, RDKit 2026.3.6, pyarrow 24.0.0), and \texttt{pyproject.toml} declares the supported ranges. Continuous integration runs the test suites on Python 3.10--3.13.

\emph{Primary corpus.} The primary corpus consists of the $414{,}992$ spectra whose \texttt{ingest\_lib} field names GNPS ($220{,}849$), MassBank ($101{,}727$) or MoNA ($92{,}416$), selected by \texttt{scripts/make\_open\_subset.py} from the aggregated CASMI 2026 file distributed by the competition organisers. Two limitations of that route must be stated rather than implied. The aggregated file carries no per-record licence column, and all three sources declare licences per record, so library membership does not establish that any individual record is redistributable. It also carries no accession, spectrum identifier or USI column, so the selected records cannot be matched back to their source entries or re-fetched from GNPS, MassBank and MoNA by identifier. Reproducing this corpus therefore currently requires the aggregated file, and assembling a redistributable equivalent would require re-deriving it from the sources with those fields retained. \texttt{docs/corpus\_composition.md} records each source library's contribution and which parts are redistributable.

\emph{Filtering and identity.} Records are retained if precursor $\mz<2{,}000$ and reported precursor error is within $20$~ppm; peaks above $0.1\%$ of the base peak and below precursor $+1.5$~Da are kept, capped at the 256 most intense. Molecular identity is the 14-character InChIKey first block, and folds are assigned by generic Murcko scaffold group as described in Methods; \texttt{scripts/split\_integrity\_audit.py} reproduces the audit reported there.

\emph{Artefacts.} The repository contains the per-seed metrics for every run, the per-target average precision for every arm and seed, the bootstrap draws, the stratified absorption counts, the trained-run configurations, and the figure- and table-generating scripts, under \texttt{results/} and \texttt{scripts/}.

\emph{Sensitivity corpus.} The in-house libraries in the CASMI aggregate are not redistributable and are not used in the primary experiments. The sensitivity corpus drawn from them cannot be reproduced by a third party, and no conclusion in this paper rests on it alone.
