\subsection*{Abstract}
\small
Comparisons between machine-learning models for tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS)
routinely place a model that reads individual peaks at full mass precision against a
baseline built on binned intensity vectors, and read the difference as evidence about
the representation. Such comparisons also change the encoder, the pooling rule, the
acquisition information each arm receives and the optimisation each arm is given, so
representation is confounded with comparator construction. We separate these sources
in MS/MS substructure prediction on a public-source corpus of $414{,}992$ spectra
from GNPS, MassBank and MoNA spanning $66$ instrument types, of which $372{,}200$ are
retained, evaluated on $10{,}058$ scaffold-disjoint held-out structures under three
seeds. An unconditioned binned baseline reaches $0.1130$ macro AUPRC against a
full-precision peak-token model's $0.1709$. Supplying the baseline with the
acquisition covariates the peak-token arms already received raises it to $0.1418$. An
architecture-specific hyperparameter search, selected on the validation fold and never
consulting the held-out fold, raises it to $0.2601 \pm 0.0008$ and reverses the
ordering. The same search budget applied to the peak-token arm's own capacity
parameters raises it from $0.1567$ to $0.1573 \pm 0.0036$ and selects the
configuration that arm already had, leaving a tuned-against-tuned difference of
$-0.1028$. Because the optimised baseline carries $6.2\times$ the parameters, this
does not establish that binned representations are superior; it establishes that the
original ordering was not robust to comparator construction. Within a fixed
peak-token encoder, by contrast, restoring full mass precision contributes $+0.0142$
($+0.0097$ to $+0.0187$), the study's one single-factor intervention. Representation
claims therefore require matched comparator inputs and architecture-appropriate
optimisation, and causal attribution should rest on within-architecture controls
wherever possible.
\normalsize

\hd{Scientific contribution}
\small
We apply three methodological controls to one MS/MS substructure-prediction ladder.
Metadata parity supplies every arm with the same acquisition covariates. Matched
search effort gives each architecture the same search budget over its own capacity
parameters. Within-encoder mass-axis quantisation moves the mass grid inside a fixed
peak-token encoder. The first two controls test comparator construction, and together
they reverse the apparent ordering between a full-precision peak-token model and a
binned baseline. The third isolates a representation change, and yields $+0.0142$
macro AUPRC with no baseline entering the contrast. The three do not estimate
comparable causal quantities and are not presented as doing so: only the third is a
single-factor intervention. The contribution is methodological: a reported gain of
this kind may not be robust to how the comparator is built and optimised.
\normalsize

\section*{Introduction}
Assigning a chemical structure to an MS/MS spectrum is a central challenge in untargeted metabolomics. Where the molecule appears in a reference library, the task can be approached by spectral matching, but many spectra acquired from biological extracts have no exact reference match \cite{hoffmann2022cosmic}. Computational annotation therefore relates a spectrum to candidate structures through in-silico fragmentation and scoring, as in MetFrag \cite{ruttkies2016metfrag}; prediction of structural fingerprints followed by database search, as in CSI:FingerID \cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid} and MIST \cite{goldman2023mist}; or learned spectral representations, as in MS2DeepScore \cite{huber2021ms2deepscore} and DreaMS \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}.

For much of the recent machine-learning literature, a spectrum has been discretised onto a fixed grid of bins and presented to a model as a dense vector. This convention originated in spectral library search, where bins wider than the instrument's mass error make matching robust to calibration drift. A $0.5$~Da bin spans roughly $1{,}700$~ppm at $\mz=300$, whereas a modern time-of-flight instrument can operate at approximately $5$~ppm. Such binning discards fine-scale information including mass defect, the difference between exact and nominal mass; for example, $\mathrm{C_5H_7^+}$ ($67.0542$) and $\mathrm{C_4H_3O^+}$ ($67.0178$) fall within the same $0.5$~Da bin despite distinct elemental compositions.

Modern systems increasingly avoid coarse binning. CSI:FingerID scores fragmentation trees over exact masses, MIST tokenises peaks by chemical formula, DreaMS operates on peak lists, and TransExION embeds nominal mass and mass defect in separate channels \cite{buithi2024transexion}. Recent work also benchmarks alternative MS/MS featurisations \cite{gine2026benchmarking} and examines how much mass resolution chemical-similarity prediction requires \cite{dejonge2026cross}.

These two studies are the most direct empirical statements available about what mass precision is worth, and they point in different directions. Gin\'e et al.\ \cite{gine2026benchmarking} compare adaptive binning and spectrum hashing against learned embeddings across a large grid of models for structure annotation, and report that retrieval degrades markedly as the mass tolerance is relaxed. Working on chemical-similarity prediction rather than structure annotation, de Jonge et al.\ \cite{dejonge2026cross} adopt $0.1$~Da bins and report that finer bins did not improve performance. The disagreement is itself a reason to ask the question in a form that admits a clean answer. Both establish that representation matters; neither isolates which part of a pipeline causes a particular gain, because a benchmark measures each featurisation bundled with the encoder that reads it and therefore estimates a joint effect at many points rather than separating it at one.

Two further strands bear directly on the present design. First, acquisition conditioning: collision energy, adduct, polarity and instrument determine which bonds break and which ions are observed, and models that receive this information as an explicit input are now common. Whether a comparator receives the same information is, as far as we are aware, not routinely reported. Second, multi-spectrum aggregation: a molecule is typically represented by several spectra acquired at different energies or in different adduct forms, and how those are combined is a modelling choice independent of how any single spectrum is encoded. Benchmarks such as MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym} have standardised evaluation and leakage control for this field, but the attribution question --- which component of a pipeline a reported gain belongs to --- is orthogonal to benchmarking and is not resolved by it.

The reason attribution is hard here is structural. A dense binned vector and a sparse, variable-length peak set require different input interfaces, so moving from one to the other commonly changes the representation and the encoder together. Additional differences enter through pooling, training protocol and acquisition metadata. A two-arm comparison therefore estimates the joint difference between complete pipelines, not the causal contribution of the spectral representation alone.

We address this attribution problem in a controlled MS/MS substructure-prediction setting. Our key control quantises the mass axis \emph{inside} the peak-token pipeline: peaks are snapped to the same $0.5$~Da grid used by the dense baseline, collisions are merged, and the resulting tokens pass through the same peak-set encoder used for full-precision peaks. This makes the M1R-to-M1 contrast a direct test of mass-axis precision while holding the peak-token architecture fixed. We also correct an acquisition-metadata imbalance in the original dense baseline. Finally, we include an attention-free token sensitivity arm and a multi-spectrum aggregation arm to place the mass-axis effect in context. Throughout, we distinguish contrasts that are single-factor interventions from those that remain composite.

This paper does not propose a state-of-the-art structure-prediction architecture, and it does not benchmark external systems. CSI:FingerID, MIST, DreaMS, MS2DeepScore and TransExION differ from this work and from each other in training data, prediction target, objective and architecture; a number produced here is not comparable to a number produced there. What the paper offers is a control strategy --- metadata parity and within-encoder mass quantisation --- and an empirical demonstration of how much a reported representation gain can move when those controls are applied.

\section*{Methods}
\hd{Corpora and splits}
Both corpora are drawn from the CASMI 2026 training corpus, an aggregation of eleven public and in-house MS/MS libraries whose \texttt{ingest\_lib} field records the source library.

The public-source primary corpus is the subset contributed by the GNPS, MassBank and MoNA libraries: $414{,}992$ spectra over $52{,}428$ structures, spanning $66$ instrument types and $112$ adducts. After filtering, $372{,}200$ spectra over $50{,}287$ structures and $24{,}627{,}256$ peaks remain for modelling. We distinguish these source and retained counts throughout. All primary results are reported on this corpus.

The secondary corpus is a contiguous slice of two of the file's twenty-one row groups, containing $262{,}144$ spectra. Because the file is ordered by source library, this slice is not a random sample: it is $99.0\%$ one in-house library and $1.0\%$ another, and $99\%$ of spectra come from a single instrument (timsTOF), across two instrument types and sixteen adducts. We use it as a deliberately contrasting sensitivity corpus. It is not an external validation set and not an independent replication: it is drawn from the same aggregate resource, processed by the same code, and is not redistributable.

Spectra were retained if precursor $\mz<2{,}000$ and the reported precursor error was within $20$~ppm. Peaks were retained above $0.1\%$ of the base peak and below the precursor plus $1.5$~Da, capped at the $256$ most intense, with $\mz$ held in double precision throughout preprocessing.

\hd{Molecular identity and the fold assignment}
Molecular identity is the first block of the InChIKey, a $14$-character hash of the connectivity layer produced by standard InChI normalisation. That block excludes the stereochemical and isotopic layers, and normalisation fixes a reference protonation and tautomeric form before hashing, so records differing only in information outside the connectivity layer share one identity key and are assigned to a single fold. Records differing within that layer do not. The same identifier also merges records duplicated across source libraries, which is the case that matters most here: GNPS, MassBank and MoNA overlap, and a compound present in two of them must not be split between fitting and evaluation. Structures are additionally represented by a canonical SMILES string, used for scaffold computation and for reporting, but identity is defined by the InChIKey block alone.

Structures were then assigned to five folds by grouping generic Murcko scaffolds, with acyclic structures falling back to grouping by their own identifier, and fold sizes balanced greedily. Folds 2--4 were used for fitting, fold 1 for model selection and fold 0 for final evaluation. This design follows the generalisation-demanding practice used in MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}, while retaining source-library selection specific to the present study.

Scaffold grouping is stricter than a random split over spectra, and stricter than a split over structures, because it prevents a model from being evaluated on a molecule whose scaffold it has already seen. It is not equivalent to chemical-space independence. Two molecules with different generic Murcko scaffolds may still be close neighbours by any pharmacophoric or fragment-level measure, and BRICS fragments in particular recur across scaffolds by construction. A split defined on a graph edit distance between structures, as used by MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym}, is stricter again; we did not adopt it here because our corpora are selected by source library rather than drawn from that benchmark.

\hd{Split-integrity audit}
We verified the split rather than asserting it. On the primary corpus, across $50{,}287$ structures and $372{,}200$ retained spectra: no molecular identifier occurred in more than one fold ($0$ of $50{,}287$); no scaffold group occurred in more than one fold ($0$ of $19{,}648$ groups, of which $7{,}456$ are acyclic structures keyed by their own identifier); every spectrum inherited the fold of its structure, with none unassigned. Fold sizes are $10{,}058$, $10{,}058$, $10{,}057$, $10{,}057$ and $10{,}057$ structures. The same audit on the sensitivity corpus is likewise clean: $0$ of $43{,}536$ identifiers and $0$ of $13{,}620$ scaffold groups span folds.

Fold 0 of the primary corpus contains $10{,}058$ structures and all $10{,}058$ are evaluated. Fold 0 of the sensitivity corpus contains $8{,}708$ structures, of which $16$ are structures recoverable from the CASMI competition test set by exact spectral duplicate matching; those are excluded from both fitting and evaluation, leaving the $8{,}692$ reported. The overlap was reported to the competition organisers, and the recovered structures were used only to remove those records from fitting and evaluation: no competition submission was derived from them. No such structures are present in the primary corpus, which is one further reason to treat it as primary. The audit is reproduced by \texttt{scripts/split\_integrity\_audit.py}.

\hd{Structural targets}
Each structure was decomposed with BRICS and represented by the presence or absence of each of the $512$ most frequent attachment-labelled fragments, with the vocabulary fitted on the training folds only. On the primary corpus the target matrix is $99.3\%$ zero with a median of four positive targets per molecule. A total of $433$ targets have at least five held-out positives, at a mean prevalence of $0.0085$, which is the chance level for macro AUPRC. The sensitivity corpus is less sparse ($97.1\%$ zero, median $17$ positive targets, $477$ scored targets, mean prevalence $0.0093$). Because the targets are highly imbalanced, macro AUPRC is the primary metric; macro AUROC, micro AUPRC and Brier score are secondary summaries.

\hd{Five-arm ablation ladder}
The five arms are ordered as M0, M1D, M1R, M1 and M2. The ladder decomposes the observed pipeline difference, but not every adjacent contrast is a single-factor intervention. M1R-to-M1 changes one component. M0-to-M1D, M1D-to-M1R and M2-to-M1 change several simultaneously and are labelled composite wherever they appear.

\emph{M0, metadata-matched dense baseline.} Each spectrum is projected onto $2{,}048$ fragment bins and $2{,}048$ precursor-minus-fragment bins of width $0.5$~Da over $[0,1024)$, with square-root intensity accumulated within bins and the concatenated $4{,}096$-dimensional vector $L_2$-normalised. A two-layer perceptron encodes the vector. Collision energy, adduct, polarity and instrument embeddings are concatenated to the dense representation. This gives M0 access to the same acquisition variables as the peak-token arms, although the conditioning mechanism is not identical: the token encoder injects them through adaptive layer normalisation. We therefore describe the corrected comparator as metadata \emph{matched}, not as mechanically identical in conditioning, and we treat the difference in mechanism as a limitation of the M1R-to-M0 contrast rather than a property of it.

\emph{M1R, rounded peak-token control.} Each fragment mass $m$ is assigned to a cell by $\lfloor m/w \rfloor$, with $w=0.5$~Da, and moved to the cell centre. Peaks sharing a cell are merged by accumulating square-root intensity, matching the accumulator used by M0. The precursor is snapped to the same grid before the neutral loss is computed, preventing fine precursor mass from leaking back into the token representation through the subtraction $m^{\mathrm{prec}}-m$. The resulting tokens pass through the same peak-token encoder used by M1. M1R and M1 have identical parameter counts ($712{,}336$) and differ only by whether mass quantisation is active. This is the principal representation control in the study.

\emph{M1D, attention-free token sensitivity arm.} M1D uses the same rounded peak masses and acquisition variables as M1R, but removes self-attention from the token blocks. Because a learned pooling token cannot aggregate independent position-wise tokens in the absence of attention, M1D pools peaks by a masked mean. Removing attention also frees parameters, so its feed-forward layers were widened, yielding $714{,}528$ parameters versus $712{,}336$ for M1R. M1D therefore tests whether the attention-bearing configuration improves on an approximately capacity-matched attention-free token model. It does \emph{not} isolate a self-attention effect, because pooling and feed-forward width change with it.

\emph{M1, full-precision peak-token model.} M1 is identical to M1R except that mass quantisation is disabled, so the model receives the instrument-reported mass precision. The M1-to-M1R contrast isolates the mass-axis precision intervention within a fixed encoder, pooling rule, training protocol and parameter count.

\emph{M2, multi-spectrum aggregation.} M2 is identical to M1 except that the mean over a molecule's spectra is replaced by the measurement model specified below. The encoder, the mass axis, the peak pooling, the training protocol and the target heads are unchanged. The contrast is nonetheless composite rather than single-factor, and we label it so throughout: the measurement model replaces M1's linear output head with a learned per-target prior, covariate-dependent scaling and a logistic--Gaussian output map, all of which act even on a molecule represented by a single spectrum, where there is nothing to aggregate. Sect.~\ref{sec:agg} reports the stratification that separates the two components empirically.

\hd{Peak encoding}
Each peak is encoded as a token. For a peak at $\mz=m$ in a spectrum with precursor mass $m^{\mathrm{prec}}$, we form a multi-scale sinusoidal embedding
\begin{equation}
\varphi_L(m)=\bigl[\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_1),\cos(2\pi m/\lambda_1),\ldots,\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_L),\cos(2\pi m/\lambda_L)\bigr]^\top,
\end{equation}
with $L=32$ wavelengths spaced geometrically from $2$~mDa to $10^3$~Da, applied to both fragment mass and neutral loss. The token also receives the explicit defect $\delta=m-\mathrm{round}(m)$, square-root and log-transformed intensity, and intensity rank. At a wavelength of $2$~mDa and $\mz=342$ the phase exceeds $10^6$, so the phase is evaluated in double precision and reduced modulo $2\pi$ before casting to single precision. Tokens are encoded by a four-block Transformer of width $128$ with eight heads, with acquisition covariates entering each block through adaptive layer normalisation.

\hd{Acquisition covariates}
For spectrum $k$ of molecule $i$, the covariate vector $c_{ik}\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$ is produced by a small encoder from learned embeddings of adduct, polarity and instrument family, a six-knot spline basis in harmonised collision energy, the log precursor mass, and an explicit indicator for missing collision energy. Energies reported as normalised collision energy are converted to eV by $\mathrm{eV}=\mathrm{NCE}\cdot \mz/500$; energies in unrecognised units are treated as missing rather than placed on the eV scale. Missing metadata are therefore represented explicitly rather than imputed, and the same $c_{ik}$ is supplied to every arm.

\hd{Multi-spectrum aggregation in M2}
Let molecule $i$ have spectra $x_{i1},\dots,x_{iK_i}$ with encoder outputs $h_{ik}\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ and covariate vectors $c_{ik}$. M2 aggregates at two levels, and we state both because the second is where the arm's parameters lie.

\emph{Acquisition-dependent reliability.} For each target $j\in\{1,\dots,512\}$ the covariates produce three quantities by rank-one affine maps of $c_{ik}$,
\begin{align}
\alpha_j(c_{ik}) &= \alpha^0_j + a_j^\top c_{ik}, \\
\beta_j(c_{ik}) &= \mathrm{softplus}\bigl(\beta^0_j + b_j^\top c_{ik}\bigr) + \varepsilon, \qquad \varepsilon = 10^{-2},\\
\log \sigma_j(c_{ik}) &= s^0_j + s_j^\top c_{ik}, \qquad \text{clamped to } [-4, 4],
\end{align}
an offset, a sensitivity and a noise scale. Positivity of $\beta_j$ is enforced by the softplus and the floor $\varepsilon$; positivity of $\sigma_j$ by the exponential, and the clamp bounds it away from zero and infinity. The per-spectrum precision for target $j$ is
\begin{equation}
w_{ikj} = \beta_j(c_{ik})^2/\sigma_j(c_{ik})^2,
\label{eq:prec}
\end{equation}
clamped above at $10^3$. Note that $w_{ikj}$ depends on the acquisition covariates only, not on $h_{ik}$, so it can be evaluated before the spectra are pooled.

\emph{Representation-level pooling.} The molecule representation is a normalised weighted mean over the observed spectra, using the precision of Eq.~\eqref{eq:prec} averaged over targets,
\begin{equation}
\bar w_{ik} = \tfrac{1}{512}\textstyle\sum_j w_{ikj}, \qquad
h_i = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K_i} \bar w_{ik}\, h_{ik}}{\sum_{l=1}^{K_i} \bar w_{il}} .
\label{eq:hpool}
\end{equation}
The weights are non-negative and, after the division, sum to one; masked positions contribute zero. M1 and the other arms use the same expression with $\bar w_{ik}\equiv 1$, that is, a masked mean.

\emph{Evidence-level pooling.} A per-spectrum evidence logit $\ell_{ikj}=(\,W h_{ik} + b\,)_j$ is combined across spectra by Gaussian-conjugate pooling. With the prior mean $\mu_{ij} = (\,W' h_i + b'\,)_j$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:hpool} and a learned per-target prior precision $\tau_j^{-2}$,
\begin{align}
S^{(1)}_{ij} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K_i} \frac{\beta_j(c_{ik})\bigl(\ell_{ikj}-\alpha_j(c_{ik})\bigr)}{\sigma_j(c_{ik})^2}, &
S^{(2)}_{ij} &= \sum_{k=1}^{K_i} w_{ikj}, \\
v_{ij} &= \bigl(\tau_j^{-2} + S^{(2)}_{ij}\bigr)^{-1}, &
m_{ij} &= v_{ij}\bigl(\mu_{ij}\tau_j^{-2} + S^{(1)}_{ij}\bigr),
\end{align}
and the reported probability uses the logistic--Gaussian approximation
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{logit}\,\hat p_{ij} = m_{ij}\big/\sqrt{1 + \tfrac{\pi}{8} v_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
Because $S^{(1)}$ and $S^{(2)}$ are sums over spectra, a molecule's spectra need not be processed in one pass: statistics accumulated over chunks give the same posterior, so no cap on $K_i$ is required. All aggregation parameters ($\alpha^0,a,\beta^0,b,s^0,s,\tau,W,b,W',b'$) are trained jointly with the encoder by the same objective; none is fitted post hoc.

\hd{Bin-width sweep}
To examine the precision scale continuously, we repeated the M1R construction at $w=0.25$, $0.1$, $0.05$ and $0.01$~Da, holding the encoder, parameter count, training schedule and seed fixed. Together with $w=0.5$ and full precision, this produces six mass-resolution settings. The sweep was run on the sensitivity corpus only and each intermediate width was trained once; it is exploratory except at its endpoints.

\hd{Training protocol}
Every arm was trained for 15 epochs with AdamW, a one-cycle learning-rate schedule peaking at $10^{-3}$, batches of 16 molecules, mixed precision and gradient clipping at 1.0. The objective was binary cross-entropy over the 512 BRICS-presence targets plus an auxiliary negative-binomial term on 88 functional-group counts. Checkpoints were selected by validation macro AUPRC on fold 1. The five ladder arms on the primary corpus and four arms on the sensitivity corpus were each trained under seeds $\{2026,7,13\}$, for 27 ladder runs. The two searches add 36 validation-only selection runs and six further runs for the two retrained arms, and the bin-width sweep adds four single-seed runs.

All arms were implemented for this study in PyTorch 2.11.0 (CUDA 12.8) under Python 3.14, with NumPy 2.4.4, pandas 2.3.3 and RDKit 2026.3.6, and trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 3070. M0 reproduces the binned convention described in the Introduction rather than any particular published system, and no external implementation was used as a comparator; every difference reported here is between arms of one codebase, which is what makes the within-architecture controls necessary rather than optional.

\hd{Hyperparameter selection, and the searches that make it symmetric}
No hyperparameter search was performed for any arm. This is not a symmetric choice and we state it plainly: the schedule above was selected for the peak-token Transformer and inherited unchanged by the dense baseline, so M0 and the token arms are not equidistant from their respective optima. Because the central claim of this paper concerns comparator fairness, a comparator that is merely untuned is a weaker instrument than the argument requires. The metadata correction reported below, worth more than any contrast in the study, is direct evidence that M0 was not near its ceiling.

We therefore prespecify a modest tuning experiment for the dense baseline. The scaffold split is fixed and unchanged; folds 2--4 are used for fitting and fold 1 for model selection; fold 0 is not consulted at any point during selection. The grid varies learning rate $\in\{3\times10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 3\times10^{-3}\}$, hidden width $\in\{256, 512, 1024\}$ and dropout $\in\{0.1, 0.3\}$, all other settings held at the values above. The configuration with the best fold-1 macro AUPRC is then trained under the three seeds and evaluated once on fold 0. The purpose is to test whether the residual M1R-to-M0 difference survives a stronger dense comparator; if the tuned baseline changes the decomposition, every contrast involving M0 must be recomputed and reported against the tuned baseline, and the contrasts not involving M0 --- M1-to-M1R and M2-to-M1 --- are unaffected by construction. The design is stated here in the prospective form in which it was fixed, before the outcome was known.

Selection runs evaluate the validation fold only. The driver refuses to proceed if a
selection run has produced a held-out score, so the search cannot be contaminated by
fold 0 even accidentally. As a check that the grid does what it claims, it
reproduces the incumbent configuration's validation score exactly at the
incumbent's own settings.

A search on one side of a contrast and not the other reverses an unfairness
rather than removing it, so the same grid was then run on the peak-token side. The
width axis maps to the knob that carries capacity in that arm --- the feed-forward
width of the peak-token blocks --- with $d_{\mathrm{model}}$ held at $128$ so that
the representation width is unchanged and the search varies capacity rather than
the interface. Learning rate and dropout ranges are identical, selection is again
on fold 1, and fold 0 is again not consulted. We call the resulting arm M1RT, and
the M1RT-to-M0T contrast is the tuned-against-tuned comparison: both sides searched
over the same grid, on the same folds, with the held-out fold consulted by neither.
It is the comparison on which Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned} rests. The design equalises
search effort over architecture-specific capacity parameters; it does not equalise
parameter count or effective capacity, and we do not treat it as doing so.

\hd{An exact attention ablation, which this study specifies and does not run}
The M1D-to-M1R contrast reported below does not price self-attention, and the reason is worth stating because it is a property of the comparison rather than of the measurement. Removing attention forces a second change. M1R pools peaks with a learned token, which is a query that aggregates by attending to them, so an arm without attention cannot use one and M1D pools by a masked mean instead. Removing attention also frees parameters, which were returned as feed-forward width. Three things therefore differ between the two arms at once, and a single measurement cannot be divided among them.

The consequence is not that the estimate is imprecise but that it is uninterpretable in a particular direction: if self-attention helped and masked-mean pooling helped by a similar amount, the two would cancel and the contrast would read as approximately zero --- which is close to what it does read. An interval containing zero is therefore consistent with attention mattering and with attention not mattering, and the design cannot separate the two.

The experiment that would price the operator holds the input, the feed-forward width, the training protocol and the parameter count fixed as closely as the operator allows, and toggles self-attention alone. Its essential feature is that \emph{both} arms pool peaks by a masked mean, so that the pooling rule no longer changes with the operator; the attention-bearing arm gives up its learned token to buy that. No quantity in this paper prices self-attention.

\hd{Evaluation and three sources of uncertainty}
Macro AUPRC is reported over targets with at least five held-out positives. Three distinct sources of variability bear on the contrasts, and we report them separately rather than combining them into one interval.

\emph{(i) Seed variability.} Each arm is trained three times under different initialisations. We report the standard deviation of each contrast across seeds, and we check every claimed ordering within each seed rather than reading it off the means.

\emph{(ii) Target-resampling uncertainty.} For paired contrasts, each target's average precision is first averaged over the three seeds; per-target differences are then computed and their mean is resampled over targets with $5{,}000$ bootstrap draws. We call the resulting intervals \emph{target-resampling intervals}. They describe heterogeneity across the scored target set at a fixed held-out set of molecules. They do \emph{not} describe uncertainty arising from which molecules were held out, and BRICS targets are not statistically independent because fragments co-occur, so these intervals are narrower than a fully independent resampling would give.

\emph{(iii) Test-set uncertainty over structures.} To quantify the sampling of held-out molecules we add a \emph{scaffold-cluster bootstrap}. Scaffold groups, not molecules or spectra, are the resampling unit, because molecules sharing a generic Murcko scaffold were assigned to a fold together and are not exchangeable with one another. Whole scaffold groups are drawn with replacement, the full macro AUPRC is recomputed per arm within each resample over the scored targets, and the contrasts are formed inside the resample so that pairing is preserved. We use $2{,}000$ draws over the $3{,}784$ scaffold clusters that partition the $10{,}058$ held-out structures.

The two resamplings vary different things and are not interchangeable. The
target-resampling bootstrap holds the held-out structures fixed and varies the target
set; the scaffold-cluster bootstrap holds the target set fixed and varies the
structural composition of the held-out set; seed spread varies initialisation alone.

Average precision is not a linear functional of the evaluation set. Resampling with
replacement duplicates structures, and duplication changes the ranking denominators
from which precision is computed, so in general
$\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{boot}}[\widehat{\Delta}^{\,*}] \neq \widehat{\Delta}$ even when
the resampling scheme is correct. A scaffold-cluster bootstrap mean that sits away
from its point estimate is therefore expected behaviour rather than evidence of an
error, and we report both quantities side by side so that the displacement can be
read directly. We use the percentile interval without bias correction and interpret
all three sources descriptively. Neither interval is a confidence interval for the
population of all possible MS/MS tasks.

\section*{Results}
Each contrast below is labelled \onefactor{} where the arms differ in exactly one component, and \composite{} where they differ in more than one. Causal language is used only for the former.

\hd{1. Metadata matching substantially reduces the apparent two-arm gap}
The initial dense baseline did not use collision energy, adduct, polarity or instrument variables, whereas the peak-token models did. Adding the same acquisition variables to M0 by concatenating their embedding to the dense spectral vector raises M0 from $0.1130$ to $0.1418$ macro AUPRC, an improvement of $+0.0288$. Consequently the apparent M1-to-M0 advantage falls from $+0.0579$ to $+0.0291$. About half of the original two-arm difference was attributable to unequal metadata availability rather than to mass precision or peak-token modelling.

At this point in the study the metadata correction was the largest single quantity measured, and it already belonged to the construction of the comparator rather than to any modelling choice under test. Sect.~\ref{sec:tuned} reports a second intervention on the comparator that is four times larger again. Because M0 and the peak-token arms inject the metadata through different mechanisms, we describe the corrected comparator as metadata matched, not as mechanically identical in conditioning. Table~\ref{tab:arms} summarises the five arms under the corrected baseline, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:comparator} places that correction beside the two that follow.

Sects.~\ref{sec:tuned} and \ref{sec:matched} take the comparator further, and are
reported next because the comparator corrections are larger than any modelling effect
that follows them. The contrasts in Sects.~\ref{sec:massaxis} onwards are measured
against this metadata-matched but \emph{untuned} baseline, which is the comparator a
conventional study would use; those that involve M0 are recomputed against the tuned

\hd{2. Architecture-specific tuning of the comparator reverses the ordering}
\hdlab{2}{sec:tuned}
The search prespecified in Methods was run: 18 configurations, fitted on folds 2--4,
selected on fold 1, fold 0 never consulted. The grid reproduced the incumbent's
validation score exactly at the incumbent's settings ($0.1702$), and selected
$\mathrm{lr}=3\times10^{-3}$, hidden width $1024$, dropout $0.3$ at a validation

tuned baseline reaches $0.2601 \pm 0.0008$ against the untuned $0.1418$

\hd{3. Matched search effort on the peak-token arm does not restore the ordering}
\hdlab{3}{sec:matched}
The same grid was then run on the peak-token side, with the width axis mapped to
the feed-forward width as specified in Methods: again 18 configurations, again
selected on fold 1, again with fold 0 never consulted. It selected
$\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$, feed-forward width $256$, dropout $0.1$ at a validation score
of $0.2026$ --- which is M1R's incumbent configuration, the settings the arm already
had. Retrained under the three seeds, the tuned arm M1RT reaches $0.1573 \pm 0.0036$

changing across seeds. Nothing measurable.

The tuned-against-tuned comparison is accordingly where the untuned one already

Three things must be said about what this does and does not show.

It is still not evidence that binned representations are superior. The tuned
baseline carries $6.2\times$ M1RT's parameters, so the comparison is not capacity
matched, and the grid is modest --- six learning-rate-by-width combinations at two
dropout rates --- with the baseline's selection on its boundary in both axes. What
can no longer be said is that the ordering reflects unequal search effort, because
the effort was equalised and the ordering did not move.

The mechanism is underfitting, not regularisation. The untuned baseline ends training
at a presence loss of $0.01885$ against the tuned arm's $0.01084$: the tuned model
fits the training data better \emph{and} generalises better, with a smaller relative
validation-to-test gap ($11.6\%$ against $15.8\%$). Its validation curve was still
rising at the final epoch under the shared schedule.

The cause is traceable to the design, which is the part that generalises beyond this
codebase. The binned encoder's hidden width was not chosen for a $4{,}096$-dimensional
input; it was set to twice $d_{\mathrm{model}}$, the peak-token encoder's width. The
comparator's only capacity parameter was a function of the treatment's
hyperparameter, and the learning-rate schedule was likewise selected for the
treatment's optimisation behaviour. A binned-versus-peak comparison can therefore be
biased before any data is seen, by inheritance rather than by intent, and this is
checkable in any codebase in minutes.

The two searches make that inheritance visible rather than inferred. The rate the
dense baseline selected, $3\times10^{-3}$, is the worst region of the grid for the
peak-token arm: all six configurations at that rate score between $0.033$ and
$0.058$ on validation, against $0.2026$ at $10^{-3}$. The width axis says the same
in reverse --- the dense baseline selects the largest width on the grid and the
peak-token arm the smallest. A single shared schedule cannot be neutral between two
arms whose optima sit at opposite corners of it, and the one this study inherited
was the treatment's.

\hd{4. Restoring mass precision within a fixed encoder gives a reproducible gain (\onefactor)}
\hdlab{4}{sec:massaxis}
Against the metadata-matched baseline, M1 reaches $0.1709$, a paired target-level gain of $+0.0291$ ($95\%$ target-resampling interval $+0.0208$ to $+0.0371$), with $72.5\%$ of targets improved. The rounding control divides that numerical difference into $+0.0149$ for M1R minus M0 and $+0.0142$ for M1 minus M1R.

M1R and M1 are identical in encoder, pooling, parameter count, schedule and seed set, and differ only in whether the mass axis is quantised to the baseline's grid. This is the study's one single-factor intervention. The gain is $+0.0142$ ($+0.0097$ to $+0.0187$), improving $64.9\%$ of targets, and it is the most reproducible contrast across seeds in the study, with a seed standard deviation of $0.0004$ against $0.0013$ for M1R minus M0 and $0.0009$ for aggregation. Mass precision therefore carries predictive information that the binned representation removes, and the size of that information is measured here without confounding by architecture.

\hd{5. The rounded token-encoder package remains a composite comparison (\composite)}
The M1R-to-M0 difference of $+0.0149$ ($+0.0080$ to $+0.0221$; $66.7\%$ of targets) is broader than an encoder change. The two arms differ in input interface (dense vector against per-peak tokens), in network family (perceptron against Transformer), in peak pooling, and in the mechanism by which acquisition covariates are injected (concatenation against adaptive layer normalisation). We therefore call it the \emph{rounded token-encoder package} difference and do not attribute it to any one of those components.

Its numerical similarity to the mass-axis increment supports a negative statement rather than a positive one: after metadata matching, the residual advantage of the full-precision peak-token model is not dominated by mass resolution. It does not establish that architecture and mass precision have equal causal effects, and the two cannot be ordered reliably --- their difference is $+0.0019$, $+0.0015$ and $-0.0012$ across the three seeds, changing sign.

\hd{6. The attention sensitivity analysis does not identify an attention effect (\composite)}
\hdlab{6}{sec:attn}
M1D minus M0 is $+0.0172$ ($+0.0102$ to $+0.0242$), and M1R minus M1D is $-0.0023$ ($-0.0066$ to $+0.0020$), an interval containing zero, with $54.3\%$ of targets improved and a positive mean in only one seed of three. The attention-bearing rounded-token model is therefore not shown to outperform the approximately capacity-matched attention-free one in this comparison.

We do not read the negative mean as evidence that attention is harmful, and we do not read the result as an attention effect at all: M1D and M1R differ in the attention operator, in peak pooling and in feed-forward width together, because an attention-free arm cannot use a learned pooling token. An interval containing zero is what two offsetting effects would also produce, so it is evidence about the package and not about the operator. We therefore describe the comparison as attention-bearing versus approximately capacity-matched attention-free configuration, and treat it as a sensitivity analysis. The defensible statement is narrow: the tested attention-bearing configuration was not necessary to reach the rounded-token performance observed here. The experiment that would identify the operator's contribution is specified in Methods and remains future work.

\hd{7. The measurement model improves prediction, and aggregation is only one component (\composite)}
\hdlab{7}{sec:agg}
Replacing the masked mean over a molecule's spectra with the measurement model of Eqs.~(2)--(9) is worth $+0.0179$ ($+0.0136$ to $+0.0223$; $63.7\%$ of targets).

This is not a pure aggregation effect. Stratifying the held-out set by the number
of spectra per molecule gives $+0.0085$ on the $3{,}443$ molecules represented by a
\emph{single} spectrum, $+0.0101$ on the $2{,}137$ with two spectra and $+0.0143$ on
the $4{,}478$ with three or four. The gain among single-spectrum molecules
demonstrates that a substantial component of M2's advantage arises from changes in
the output and evidence model that operate even when multi-spectrum aggregation is
impossible: at $K_i=1$, Eqs.~(7)--(9) still apply a learned per-target prior,
covariate-dependent scaling and the logistic--Gaussian output map, none of which
M1's linear head has.

The increasing gain with the number of spectra is consistent with an additional
contribution from aggregation, but does not isolate that contribution causally. The
three strata are observationally different populations of molecules, not randomised
assignments of spectrum count, so the differences between them confound the number
of spectra with whatever else distinguishes molecules measured once from molecules
measured repeatedly. M2~$-$~M1 is reported throughout as a composite contrast, and
no split of the $+0.0179$ into calibration and aggregation components is claimed.

The monotone trend across strata is consistent with a genuine aggregation component
on top of that, and it carries a second implication: the evaluation uses at most
four spectra per molecule, while $2{,}755$ of the $10{,}058$ held-out molecules have
more available. The reported figure is therefore measured under a cap that
Eqs.~(7)--(8) do not require, and probably understates what the pooling contributes.

It exceeds the M1R-to-M0 package in all three seeds by $+0.0009$, $+0.0030$ and $+0.0049$, and the mass-axis intervention in all three by $+0.0028$, $+0.0045$ and $+0.0037$; the same direction against the package holds in all three seeds on the sensitivity corpus. With three seeds per corpus we treat this as a consistent empirical ordering in these runs rather than an established general ranking. Its relevance to the paper's argument is that a component with nothing to do with spectral representation produces an effect of the same magnitude as the representation change being studied.

\hd{9. Peak absorption describes the information coarse binning removes}
On the primary corpus's held-out spectra, $23.6\%$ of the $3{,}797{,}166$ peaks fall into a $0.5$~Da bin already occupied by another peak from the same spectrum and are therefore absorbed. The corresponding fraction on the sensitivity corpus's held-out spectra is $34.4\%$. At $0.01$~Da the reported held-out absorption fractions fall to $2.9\%$ and $6.5\%$ respectively. Absorption is reported throughout on each corpus's own held-out spectra; the same quantity computed over all retained spectra is $37.5\%$ rather than $34.4\%$ on the sensitivity corpus, and we quote the held-out figure because it matches the set on which the effects are measured.

A merged peak also loses compositional discrimination: enumerating plausible CHNOS compositions within $10$~ppm places $328$ candidate fragment formulae inside a single $0.5$~Da bin at $\mz=300$, consistent with the dependence of formula ambiguity on mass tolerance described by Kind and Fiehn \cite{kind2007seven}.

The corpus that loses more peaks at $0.5$~Da is also the corpus in which the mass-axis increment is larger ($34.4\%$ and $+0.0268$ versus $23.6\%$ and $+0.0142$). This association is descriptive and does not identify a mechanism; with two corpora it cannot. One plausible explanation is that the sensitivity corpus is almost entirely high-resolution timsTOF data whereas the primary corpus spans instruments of varying resolution, but that remains a hypothesis until the mass-axis increment is stratified directly by instrument resolution.

\hd{11. Target and scaffold resampling quantify different sources of uncertainty}
The target-resampling intervals above hold the held-out set fixed. The scaffold-cluster bootstrap resamples it: $2{,}000$ draws over the $3{,}784$ scaffold clusters partitioning the $10{,}058$ held-out structures, recomputing macro AUPRC per arm and forming the contrasts within each draw.

Table~\ref{tab:scaffold} gives the result. The estimator was verified before use:
run against the full held-out set it reproduces all seven reported point estimates
exactly, which is the check that the resample estimates the same quantity as the
reported metric. Because average precision is nonlinear under duplication, the
bootstrap mean is not expected to coincide with the point estimate, and the two are
shown together rather than one standing for the other.

Two features of this table bear on how the paper's claims should be read. First,
the two contrasts measured inside a fixed peak encoder and a fixed peak-pooling
rule --- the mass-axis intervention and the measurement model --- are well behaved
under resampling of structures: their bootstrap means sit within $0.0009$ and

the table, and their intervals exclude zero comfortably. Second, every contrast
whose arms differ in peak pooling or in network family is biased upward, and by
more the further apart the arms are --- $+0.0034$ for M1R~$-$~M1D, $+0.0026$ for

replacement duplicates structures, which sharpens macro AUPRC; that sharpening
cancels only between arms that rank molecules similarly. Contrasts across
architectures are therefore less stable under this resampling than contrasts
inside one, which is consistent with the reading given to them throughout.

Neither is a confidence interval for the population of MS/MS tasks. Reported together with the seed standard deviations, they say that the mass-axis intervention is reproducible across all three sources, and that the ordering of the mass-axis and token-encoder-package increments is not.

\section*{Discussion}
The central result is not that a binned representation outperforms peak tokens. It is that the apparent cross-architecture ordering is not robust to comparator construction. A two-arm comparison between the original unconditioned binned baseline and the full-precision peak-token model yields $+0.0579$ macro AUPRC. Two interventions on the comparator alone, neither touching the treatment, remove it. Supplying the dense baseline with the acquisition covariates the peak-token models already had accounts for $+0.0288$. Giving it its own hyperparameter search accounts for $+0.1183$ more, and reverses the ordering: every peak-token arm then sits below the baseline in every seed. The same grid run on the peak-token side does not restore it, being worth $+0.0006$ there, so the reversal is not an artefact of searching one arm only.

The sequence is worth stating as a chain, because each step licenses a different
statement. In the original comparison the peak-token pipeline appears better.
Matching acquisition information substantially reduces the gap. Architecture-specific
optimisation of the comparator reverses the ordering. Matched search effort on the
peak-token arm does not restore it. The capacity mismatch that remains prevents a
representation-level winner from being declared. Independently of all four steps,
quantising mass within a fixed peak-token architecture isolates a reproducible
contribution of full mass precision.

Neither comparator intervention is exotic. Metadata parity is a matter of passing the
same inputs to both sides. A per-arm hyperparameter search is standard practice that
was not applied in the original design, and its omission was not neutral, because the
shared schedule and the comparator's width both came from the treatment arm. Running
the same search budget on both sides shows how unequal that inheritance was: the
learning rate the comparator selects is the worst region of the grid for the
treatment, and the width each selects sits at the opposite end from the other's. This
result indicates that a study can satisfy the usual expectations --- identical data,
identical splits, identical objective, identical training budget, three seeds --- and
still report an ordering that inverts under a control it did not run.

Within the peak-token architecture, the rounding control gives the one contrast in which a single component moves: restoring full mass precision contributes $+0.0142$, measured with the encoder, pooling, parameter count and schedule held fixed. Mass precision is therefore not unimportant --- it carries real predictive information, measured here without architectural confounding, and it is the most reproducible effect in the study across seeds, targets and structures alike.

The remaining $+0.0149$ should not be described as an encoder effect. M1R and M0 differ in input interface, network family, peak pooling and conditioning mechanism simultaneously. The robust conclusion is therefore not that encoder and mass axis are equal, but that the apparent full-precision advantage is not dominated by mass resolution once the comparator receives the same acquisition variables.

The attention-free sensitivity arm supports a similarly narrow reading. M1R does not outperform M1D, but the arms differ in pooling and feed-forward width in addition to the attention operator. The experiment shows that the tested Transformer-style attention-bearing configuration is not necessary to obtain the rounded-token performance observed here; it does not identify the isolated effect of self-attention, and we have specified the ablation that would.

These distinctions matter because the purpose of the study is attribution. When representation, interface, architecture, pooling and metadata availability move together, a performance increment belongs to the whole package unless the design contains an intervention that changes only one component. The rounding control satisfies that requirement for mass-axis precision, and it is the only contrast here that does. The measurement-model arm does not: part of its increment appears on molecules represented by a single spectrum, where there is nothing to aggregate, so it bundles a change of output and evidence model with the change of pooling rule. The metadata correction establishes a separate and practically important point: comparator \emph{inputs} can account for a performance difference as large as the modelling changes being studied.

\hd{Relation to other work}
The systems this work sits alongside are not benchmarked here, and could not be without a different study design. CSI:FingerID \cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid}, MIST \cite{goldman2023mist}, DreaMS \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}, MS2DeepScore \cite{huber2021ms2deepscore} and TransExION \cite{buithi2024transexion} differ from this work and from one another in training corpus, prediction target, objective and architecture; a macro AUPRC over BRICS fragments is not comparable to a retrieval rank or a similarity score. Their datasets, prediction targets, evaluation metrics, architectures and objectives all differ from ours and from one another, and this study makes no claim to outperform any of them. It is an attribution and control study, not a leaderboard entry, and it does not establish why any of those systems outperforms its own baseline.

What it offers those systems is a control strategy and an empirical warning. Featurisation benchmarks such as Gin\'e et al.\ \cite{gine2026benchmarking} measure each representation bundled with the encoder that reads it; that is the right design for choosing a pipeline and the wrong one for attributing a gain, and the rounding control is a cheap way to add the missing arm. Benchmarks such as MassSpecGym \cite{bushuiev2024massspecgym} have standardised splits and leakage control, which is a different axis from attribution and complementary to it: a leakage-controlled split makes a number trustworthy, while metadata parity and a within-encoder control make it interpretable. For metadata-conditioned models specifically, our result implies that reporting what the comparator was told is as important as reporting what the model was told. For multi-spectrum aggregation, the $+0.0179$ contributed by the measurement model here is a reminder that a component unrelated to spectral representation can produce an effect of the same size as the representation change under study, and should not be left uncontrolled in a comparison whose conclusion concerns representation.

\hd{Interpretation of the sensitivity corpus}
The contrasting corpus changes the acquisition regime substantially, which is why it is informative, but it is not an independent replication. The larger mass-axis increment on the nearly all-timsTOF corpus is compatible with the hypothesis that preserving reported mass precision is more valuable when the instrument genuinely supplies high-resolution measurements. That explanation is plausible and untested here; the appropriate next analysis stratifies the M1-minus-M1R effect by instrument-resolution class using the saved held-out predictions, which requires no retraining.

The bin-width sweep is likewise suggestive rather than definitive. It indicates that a $0.01$~Da grid recovers much of, but not all of, the full-precision advantage on the sensitivity corpus. This differs below $0.1$~Da from the result reported by de Jonge et al.\ \cite{dejonge2026cross}. The tasks, architectures and training stochasticity differ, and the present sweep has one seed at each intermediate width, so neither study alone settles a universal sufficient mass resolution.

\hd{Limitations}
\emph{A. Search and capacity.} The two searches equalise search effort over
architecture-specific capacity parameters; they do not equalise parameter count or
effective capacity. The tuned dense baseline carries $4{,}442{,}128$ parameters
against the tuned peak-token arm's $712{,}336$, a factor of $6.2$, and the two grids
themselves span different capacity ranges. The grid is modest, six
learning-rate-by-width combinations at two dropout rates, and the dense baseline's
selection lies on its boundary in both axes, which indicates that the search may not
have located that architecture's optimum; the direction of any change under a broader
search cannot be assumed. The peak-token arm's search selected the configuration it
already had, which indicates that the arm was near its optimum on this grid but not
that a wider grid would leave it there. Only M1R was searched on the peak-token side;
M1, M1D and M2 inherit its configuration.

\emph{B. Statistical uncertainty.} Three seeds per arm are sufficient to show the
observed seed-to-seed pattern but not to establish rankings among small effects. The
target-resampling bootstrap does not capture uncertainty in which structures were
held out; the scaffold-cluster bootstrap addresses that but holds the target set
fixed. BRICS targets are correlated because fragments co-occur, so target-resampling
intervals are narrower than fully independent resampling would give. Average
precision is nonlinear under duplication, so bootstrap means need not coincide with
point estimates, and the percentile intervals reported here are not bias corrected. A
scaffold-cluster jackknife or a weighted bootstrap would test how far the intervals
depend on that choice; neither was run. Only one scaffold partition was used, so no
interval here covers the choice of fold assignment, and none covers the population of
MS/MS tasks.

\emph{C. Ablation purity.} Only M1-to-M1R changes one component. M1D-to-M1R is not a
pure attention contrast, because the peak-pooling rule and the feed-forward width
change with the operator; it is reported as a sensitivity analysis. M1R-to-M0 is
composite in four respects at once, including the mechanism by which metadata are
injected; a dense baseline conditioned through adaptive normalisation rather than
concatenation would test whether the $+0.0149$ depends on that mechanism. M2-to-M1 is
composite: the measurement model changes the output and evidence model as well as the
pooling rule, and a substantial part of its increment is present on molecules with a
single spectrum. Evaluation also uses at most four spectra per molecule, although
$2{,}755$ of the $10{,}058$ held-out molecules have more, so the reported figure
understates what pooling could contribute under a larger cap; the claim in Methods
that the additive sufficient statistics impose no cap on $K_i$ describes the
estimator rather than the experiment run here.

\emph{D. Data.} The sensitivity corpus is a deliberately contrasting in-house slice
of the same aggregate resource, processed by the same code. It is neither external
validation nor independent replication, and it is not redistributable, so that arm of
the study cannot be reproduced by a third party from public data. The comparator
searches were run on the primary corpus only, so the reversal reported in
Sects.~\ref{sec:tuned} and~\ref{sec:matched} is untested on the sensitivity corpus.
The instrument-resolution explanation for the difference between corpora is not
tested directly. The primary corpus is assembled from public sources and its
selection is scripted, but the aggregated file carries no per-record licence field,
so redistributability cannot be established record by record.

\emph{E. Endpoint.} The endpoint is BRICS substructure prediction over 512 frequent
fragments. The study makes no claim about candidate ranking, full molecular
fingerprints or de-novo structure elucidation, and transfer to those endpoints
remains to be demonstrated.

\section*{Conclusion}

This study reports conclusions at three levels.

\emph{Comparator robustness.} The apparent advantage of a full-precision peak-token
model over a binned baseline was not robust to comparator construction. On the
primary corpus an unconditioned $0.5$~Da binned baseline reaches $0.1130$ macro
AUPRC against the peak-token model's $0.1709$. Supplying the baseline with collision
energy, adduct, polarity and instrument raises it to $0.1418$. An
architecture-specific search of its own raises it to $0.2601 \pm 0.0008$ and reverses
the ordering. The same search budget applied to the peak-token arm's own capacity
parameters raises it from $0.1567$ to $0.1573 \pm 0.0036$ and selects the
configuration that arm already had, leaving the tuned-against-tuned difference at
$-0.1028$. Because the optimised dense baseline carries $6.2\times$ the parameters of
the optimised peak-token arm, this reversal does not establish that binned
representations are superior.

\emph{Controlled representation effect.} Within a fixed peak-token architecture,
restoring full mass precision contributes $+0.0142$ macro AUPRC ($+0.0097$ to
$+0.0187$), with the encoder, pooling rule, parameter count and schedule held fixed.
This is the study's only single-factor intervention and its most reproducible effect
across seeds, targets and held-out structures. The statement stands independently of
how the binned baseline is built, because no binned baseline enters it. The
measurement model contributes $+0.0179$ on the same comparator-free footing, but that
figure bundles multi-spectrum pooling with a change of output and evidence model and
is reported as a composite contrast.

\emph{Methodological recommendation.} Cross-architecture representation studies
should supply identical auxiliary information to every arm; give each architecture
its own reasonable optimisation over its own capacity parameters; report parameter
counts and capacity differences explicitly; and use within-architecture controls when
making causal claims about representation. In this study the comparator corrections
were larger than the modelling effects they were meant to contextualise: $+0.0288$
for metadata parity and $+0.1183$ for optimisation, against $+0.0142$ for the mass
axis and $+0.0179$ for the measurement model. The comparator's capacity was moreover
inherited from the treatment, its hidden width set to twice the peak-token encoder's
model dimension and its schedule chosen for the treatment's optimisation behaviour,
so the asymmetry was present in the design before any data was seen.

These conclusions concern experimental attribution rather than a universal ranking of
binned and peak-level MS/MS representations, and they are specific to this endpoint,
these corpora and these architectures.
