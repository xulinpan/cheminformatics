\subsection*{Abstract}
\small
Machine-learning methods for tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) almost always consume
the spectrum as a fixed-width binned intensity vector, a representation inherited
from spectral library search. Binning is rarely treated as a modelling decision and
its cost has not been measured. Here we price it directly. Using the CASMI 2026
corpus we trained two models that differ only in how the spectrum enters: a
$0.5$~Da binned vector, and a set of peaks whose $m/z$ is retained at full
precision and embedded with an explicit mass-defect channel. Data, structural
targets, optimiser, training budget and evaluation were held fixed, and the binned
model was given $63\%$ more parameters. On $8{,}692$ held-out molecules the
peak-set representation raised macro AUPRC from $0.0476$ to $0.1119$ against a
chance level of $0.0093$, a paired per-target gain of $+0.0644$ ($95\%$ CI
$+0.0570$ to $+0.0722$) that improved $89.7\%$ of targets individually. The
representation change was more than twice the size of an architectural change
(hierarchical multi-view pooling) measured on the same data. Binning at $0.5$~Da
absorbs $60.7\%$ of the observed test peaks into bins already occupied by another
peak, spans $1{,}667$~ppm at $m/z\,300$ on an instrument delivering about $5$~ppm,
and pools hundreds of candidate fragment compositions into one coordinate. We
conclude that a binned baseline is no longer an adequate control for MS/MS
structure annotation. Code and configurations are available at
\url{https://github.com/xulinpan/cheminformatics}.
\normalsize

\hd{Scientific contribution}
\small
We provide the first controlled price for the spectral input representation in
MS/MS substructure prediction, isolating it from architecture, capacity and
training budget, and granting the weaker representation the larger parameter count.
The measured effect exceeds that of the architectural component built on top of it
by a factor of two, and we quantify the mechanism by which binning destroys
information: $60.7\%$ of observed peaks are absorbed into shared bins, discarding
the mass defect that distinguishes isobaric fragment compositions. The practical
consequence is a change in what counts as an adequate control in this field.
\normalsize

\section*{Introduction}

Assigning a chemical structure to an MS/MS spectrum is the rate-limiting step in
untargeted metabolomics. Where the molecule is present in a reference library the
problem reduces to spectral matching, but most spectra acquired from biological
extracts are not \cite{duhrkop2021cosmic}. For the remainder, computational
annotation proceeds by relating the spectrum to candidate structures: by in-silico
fragmentation and scoring, as in MetFrag \cite{ruttkies2016metfrag}; by predicting
a molecular fingerprint and searching a structure database, as in CSI:FingerID
\cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid} and MIST \cite{goldman2023mist}; or by learning a
spectrum representation whose geometry reflects chemical similarity, as in
MS2DeepScore \cite{huber2021ms2deepscore} and DreaMS \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}.

Nearly all of these systems begin from the same input object. The spectrum, which is
physically a sparse list of $(m/z, \text{intensity})$ pairs, is discretised onto a
fixed grid of bins, typically $0.01$ to $1$~Da wide, and handed to the model as a
dense vector. The convention is so widespread that it is seldom stated as a choice.

Its origin lies in library search. Cosine similarity between two binned vectors is
inexpensive, and a bin comfortably wider than the instrument's mass error makes
matching robust to calibration drift between two laboratories. Those are appropriate
properties for comparing two measurements of the same compound. They are not
obviously appropriate for inferring an unknown structure, and the two problems have
been treated as though a single representation serves both. A $0.5$~Da bin spans
roughly $1{,}700$~ppm at $m/z\,300$ while a modern time-of-flight instrument
delivers on the order of $5$~ppm; everything the instrument resolves within that
window is discarded. What is discarded is not noise. The mass defect, the difference
between a fragment's exact mass and its nominal mass, is the quantity that
distinguishes $\mathrm{C_5H_7^+}$ ($67.0542$) from $\mathrm{C_4H_3O^+}$ ($67.0178$),
and therefore the quantity that carries elemental composition.

Parts of the field have moved away from naive binning. CSI:FingerID scores against
fragmentation trees built from exact masses, MIST tokenises peaks by chemical
formula, DreaMS operates on peak lists, and TransExION explicitly separates nominal
mass from an embedded mass defect when aligning fragments between spectra
\cite{buithi2024transexion}. These are strong systems, and each differs from a
binned baseline in architecture, training data and objective simultaneously. None
of them isolates the representation, so the field has no estimate of what the choice
is worth on its own, and binned baselines continue to appear as controls in new work.

This paper supplies that estimate. We train two models that differ in exactly one
respect --- whether the spectrum is discretised --- while holding the corpus,
structural targets, splits, optimiser, schedule and evaluation fixed, and we give
the binned model the larger parameter budget so that any deficit cannot be
attributed to capacity. We report the effect size with paired intervals over
targets, quantify the information binning removes, and compare the result against an
architectural change measured under the same protocol.

\section*{Methods}

\hd{Corpus and splits}
We used the CASMI 2026 training corpus, an aggregation of eleven public and
in-house MS/MS libraries. All experiments here use a fixed subset of two of its
twenty-one Parquet row groups: $262{,}041$ spectra over $43{,}536$ distinct
structures, comprising $15{,}299{,}995$ peaks after cleaning. Spectra were retained
if the precursor $m/z$ was below $2{,}000$ and the reported precursor error was
within $20$~ppm; peaks were retained above $0.1\%$ of the base peak and below the
precursor plus $1.5$~Da, capped at the $256$ most intense, and $m/z$ values were
kept in double precision throughout preprocessing.

Structures were assigned to five folds by grouping generic Murcko scaffolds, with
acyclic structures falling back to grouping by their own identifier. Fold sizes were
balanced greedily ($8{,}707$ to $8{,}708$ molecules). Folds 2--4 were used for
fitting, fold 1 for model selection, and fold 0 was held out; all reported numbers
are on fold 0 ($8{,}692$ molecules).

\hd{Structural targets}
Each structure was decomposed with BRICS and represented by the presence or absence
of each of the $512$ most frequent attachment-labelled fragments, the vocabulary
being fitted on the training folds alone. The resulting target matrix is $97.1\%$
zero, with a median of $17$ positive targets per molecule. On the held-out fold
$477$ targets carry at least five positives, at a mean prevalence of $0.0093$; that
prevalence is the chance level for macro AUPRC. Because $475$ of $600$ candidate
targets fall below $1\%$ prevalence, we report AUPRC as the primary metric and AUROC
only as a secondary summary.

\hd{Representation A: binned vector (control)}
Each spectrum was projected onto $2{,}048$ fragment bins and $2{,}048$
precursor-minus-fragment bins of width $0.5$~Da over $[0, 1024)$, with square-root
intensity accumulated into each bin and the concatenated vector $L_2$-normalised.
The $4{,}096$-dimensional vector was encoded by a two-layer perceptron. This is the
representation used, with minor variation in bin width, throughout the MS/MS
machine-learning literature.

\hd{Representation B: full-precision peak set}
Each peak was encoded as a token without discretisation. For a peak at $m/z$ $m$ in
a spectrum with precursor $m^{\mathrm{prec}}$ we formed a multi-scale sinusoidal
embedding
\begin{equation}
\varphi_L(m) = \bigl[\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_1), \cos(2\pi m/\lambda_1), \dots,
\sin(2\pi m/\lambda_L), \cos(2\pi m/\lambda_L)\bigr]^{\top},
\label{eq:fourier}
\end{equation}
with $L = 32$ wavelengths spaced geometrically from $2$~mDa to $10^{3}$~Da, applied
both to $m$ and to the neutral loss $m^{\mathrm{prec}} - m$. Short wavelengths
resolve the mass defect and long wavelengths carry nominal mass. The token also
received the explicit defect $\delta = m - \mathrm{round}(m)$, square-root and
log-transformed intensity, and intensity rank.

One numerical detail is essential rather than incidental. At a wavelength of
$2$~mDa and $m/z\,342$ the phase in Eq.~\eqref{eq:fourier} exceeds $10^{6}$, and
evaluating it in single precision destroys exactly the low-order information the
embedding exists to preserve. We therefore compute the phase in double precision and
reduce it modulo $2\pi$ before casting. With this reduction the embeddings of
$\mathrm{C_4H_3O^+}$ and $\mathrm{C_5H_7^+}$, which share a $0.5$~Da bin, differ by
$1.82$ in the infinity norm, and two peaks $1$~mDa apart at $m/z\,342$ remain
distinguishable.

Tokens were encoded by a four-block Transformer over the peak set with a learned
pooling token, each block conditioned on the acquisition covariates (adduct,
polarity, instrument family, harmonised collision energy) by adaptive layer
normalisation. The encoder is permutation invariant and accepts variable peak
counts.

\hd{Training protocol}
Both models were trained for fifteen epochs with the AdamW optimiser, a one-cycle
schedule with peak learning rate $10^{-3}$, batches of $16$ molecules, mixed
precision, and gradient clipping at $1.0$. The loss was binary cross-entropy over
the $512$ presence targets, with an auxiliary negative-binomial term on $88$
functional-group counts. Checkpoints were selected on validation macro AUPRC. No
hyperparameter search was performed for either arm. The binned model has
$1{,}164{,}560$ parameters and the peak-set model $712{,}336$: the control carries
$63\%$ more capacity than the treatment.

\hd{Evaluation}
We report macro AUPRC over targets with at least five held-out positives, together
with macro AUROC, micro AUPRC and Brier score. Because the two arms are evaluated on
identical molecules and identical targets, the appropriate contrast is paired: for
each target we compute the difference in average precision and bootstrap the mean of
those differences over targets ($5{,}000$ resamples). We additionally report the
fraction of targets improved, which is insensitive to a few large per-target swings.

\section*{Results}

\hd{What binning removes}
We first quantified the information discarded before any model is fitted
(Fig.~\ref{fig:representation}). Across the $400{,}707$ peaks of the evaluation
spectra, $243{,}273$ --- $60.7\%$ --- fall into a $0.5$~Da bin already occupied by
another peak from the same spectrum and are therefore absorbed. The loss is not
concentrated in any mass range, running between $52\%$ and $67\%$ across the
observed $m/z$ window. The observed mass defect spans $[-0.47, +0.47]$~Da and shows
clear compositional structure, most visibly the discrete combs below $m/z\,150$
where few elemental compositions are available. Enumerating plausible CHNOS
compositions within $10$~ppm places $328$ candidate fragment formulas inside a
single $0.5$~Da bin at $m/z\,300$.

\hd{Controlled comparison}
Replacing the binned vector with the full-precision peak set raised macro AUPRC from
$0.0476$ to $0.1119$, from $5.1$ to $12.0$ times the chance level of $0.0093$
(Table~\ref{tab:table2_ablation}, Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation}). Macro AUROC rose from $0.7832$
to $0.8734$, micro AUPRC from $0.2905$ to $0.3842$, and the Brier score improved
from $0.00704$ to $0.00653$. The deficit cannot be attributed to capacity, since the
binned model holds the larger parameter count.

\hd{The representation effect exceeds the architectural effect}
To place the result on a scale, we measured an architectural change under the same
protocol: replacing mean pooling over a molecule's spectra with a hierarchical
measurement model that weights each spectrum by a learned, acquisition-dependent
precision. That change is worth $+0.0341$ average precision ($95\%$ CI $+0.0277$ to
$+0.0407$), improving $72.3\%$ of targets. The representation change is therefore
$1.9$ times larger than the architectural change built on top of it, measured on the
same molecules with the same targets.

\hd{The comparison is not confounded by convergence}
Both arms plateaued well inside the training budget (Fig.~\ref{fig:training}).
Validation macro AUPRC for the binned model moved from $0.04504$ at epoch 12 to
$0.04540$ at epoch 14, and for the peak-set model from $0.10645$ to $0.10670$ over
the same interval. Neither arm is undertrained relative to the other, and extending
the schedule would not change the ordering.

\section*{Discussion}

The result is a single number with a narrow interval, and its interest lies in what
it is a number \emph{about}. Discretising the $m/z$ axis is not a preprocessing
detail on the way to a model; on this corpus it costs more than the model.

The reason binning persists is worth stating plainly, because it is a good reason
that has outlived its context. In library search a wide bin buys robustness: two
laboratories measuring the same compound will not agree to the fourth decimal place,
and a bin wider than their disagreement makes the match succeed. That argument is
about tolerance between two measurements. It says nothing about how much
information a single measurement carries, and structure annotation is an inference
problem rather than a matching problem. When the same representation was carried
across, the tolerance argument came with it and the information argument was never
made.

Our finding is consistent with the direction the strongest systems have taken
independently. CSI:FingerID scores against fragmentation trees over exact masses
\cite{duhrkop2015csifingerid}, MIST tokenises by chemical formula
\cite{goldman2023mist}, DreaMS learns from peak lists \cite{bushuiev2025dreams}, and
TransExION embeds the mass defect as a channel distinct from nominal mass
\cite{buithi2024transexion}. Each of those systems differs from a binned baseline in
several respects at once, so none of them prices the representation alone; what we
add is the controlled estimate, and the observation that it is large.

The practical consequence is a claim about controls. A new method evaluated against
a binned baseline inherits a handicap in its comparator that may exceed the effect
it reports. On the evidence here, a difference of the size typically claimed for an
architectural contribution can be produced by changing the input representation and
nothing else. We would suggest that a binned baseline is no longer sufficient, and
that methods papers in this area report their comparator's representation as
explicitly as its architecture.

\hd{Limitations}
Three limits bound the claim. The experiments use approximately a tenth of the
available corpus; while we do not expect the ordering to invert, absolute values
will move with scale. Each arm was trained once, and the reported intervals are
bootstraps over targets, which capture target-to-target variation but not
initialisation or run-to-run variance; replication across seeds is required before
the effect size is treated as settled. And the comparison uses one target family
(BRICS substructure presence), one instrument family and one bin width; a narrower
bin would recover part of the loss, and quantifying the effect as a function of bin
width would sharpen the recommendation. We report the result as a lower bound on
the cost of a widely used bin width rather than as a characterisation of binning in
general.

\section*{Conclusion}

Binned intensity vectors entered MS/MS machine learning from spectral library
search, where a bin wider than the instrument's error is a virtue. Carried into
structure annotation, the same choice discards $60.7\%$ of observed peaks and with
them the mass defect that identifies fragment compositions. In a controlled
comparison holding data, targets, optimiser and schedule fixed, and giving the
binned arm $63\%$ more parameters, replacing the binned vector with a full-precision
peak set raised macro AUPRC from $0.0476$ to $0.1119$ --- a paired gain of $+0.0644$
that improved $89.7\%$ of targets and exceeded the architectural change measured on
the same data by a factor of $1.9$. We conclude that the input representation should
be treated as a modelling decision with a measurable cost, and that a binned
baseline is no longer an adequate control for this task.
